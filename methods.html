Methodology & Sources

EpochArc separates AI history into facts, sources, impact assessments, and forecasts. This page explains how entries are selected, scored, challenged, and resolved over time.

Editorial scope

EpochArc is not a paper database or a real-time news feed. Each published milestone must answer three questions: what happened, where the evidence is, and why it changed the long arc of AI.

Historical events, impact assessments, and forecasts are handled separately. Facts must be verifiable, assessments must be evidence-linked, and forecasts must define how they will be judged later.

Significance levels

L1 Important record: useful for long-term context, but not necessarily a turning point on its own.
L2 Key event: clearly pushed research paradigms, product adoption, or industry structure.
L3 Turning point: changed public perception, capital allocation, or the mainstream technical trajectory.

L3 is intentionally rare. A new event is not treated as a turning point just because it is loud; it needs evidence of durable impact beyond a narrow technical audience.

Source standards

Tier 1 · Primary sources Papers, official announcements, model cards, repositories, laws, court filings, and technical reports.
Tier 2 · Independent confirmation Reliable reporting, institutional reports, academic reviews, and regulatory analysis used to confirm impact and adoption.
Tier 3-4 · Analysis and discovery signals Expert blogs, newsletters, HN, Reddit, and social discussion can surface candidates, but they cannot independently support a published conclusion.

Scores and consensus

Impact Score is a 0-10 display score combining significance, affected dimensions, evidence grade, and durability. It is not a claim of mathematical certainty and does not replace source evidence.

Consensus is labeled as broad, debated, or emerging. Controversy is not hidden; it tells readers where the fact, meaning, attribution, or long-term consequences remain contested.

Forecast resolution

Forecasts must include a time window, supporting signals, counter-signals, achieved criteria, and not-achieved criteria. A closed demo, a single benchmark result, or an unverifiable rumor should not count as resolution.

Reader votes represent audience expectations only. They do not change historical scores and are not treated as evidence.

Open data and limitations

The main timeline, forecast cards, and source index remain available as public JSON for review, citation, and future migration to a CMS or database. Before wider launch, source URLs, archive links, and forecast resolution fields should be completed.

EpochArc is a structured curation project, not an academic database, investment advice, or a regulatory conclusion. Entries may be revised as evidence improves.

November 4, 2026 Public Notice of Security Incident

We are writing to notify you of a data security incident that we detected on November 2, 2026. We value your trust and want to keep you informed.

What happened

On October 24, an unauthorized party used a compromised KERNEL API key to probe for access.

On October 27, the same party used the compromised key to gain access to a staff login to our logging provider. The unauthorized party accessed and downloaded data from this logging provider. Data contained a small number of kernel API keys and app deployments’ third-party secrets.

On November 3, 2026, the same party used a compromised credential for our hosting provider account to gain access to 4 GPU-enabled servers in our infrastructure. 3 of those servers ran KERNEL browser sessions and 1 server contained no customer data.

From November 2 to November 4, KERNEL employed our security incident response plan to identify, contain, eradicate, and recover all affected systems.

The breach impacted 0.28% of Kernel’s customers.

What we have done

We revoked the compromised credentials, removed the party's access to our logging provider, blocked the IP addresses involved, and took the affected servers offline. We’ve rotated all credentials those servers had access to and audited potential unauthorized access across all systems. We've notified the organizations whose data was exposed and provided guidance on remediation. See the timeline below for our incident response and other relevant key dates.

What we know

Our evidence suggests that the attacker(s) are financially motivated and this was a part of a larger cryptomining campaign. We believe they are leveraging AI-assisted pentesting platform ARTEX as a command center to automate and amplify the impact of their campaign.

We have been sharing the known and confirmed threat intelligence from this incident with abuse teams at the hosting providers and our logging infra vendor. We plan to share more with threat intelligence communities. If you need, we are able to share the technical fingerprint of the attacker. Reach out to security@kernel.sh to request it.

Next steps

We are committed to the security of our customers and safeguarding the data they trust us with. In the wake of this incident, we are doing the following:

Product Security

Increasing adoption of
scoped API keys
across our customers

Improving network control for usage of API keys

Improving API key rotation mechanisms

Detection and response

Bolstering our Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) with detection and threat intelligence from this specific threat actor

Investing in dynamic blocking rules to target emerging and fast-moving threat actors

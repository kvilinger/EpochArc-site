Title: Florida asks for order to halt ChatGPT development

URL Source: https://www.axios.com/2026/09/28/florida-openai-chatgpt-injunction-uthmeier

Published Time: 2026-09-28T14:16:10.474717Z

Markdown Content:
Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier has asked for a temporary injunction against [OpenAI](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/18/openai-pause-astra-preparedness-framework) and ChatGPT, claiming the company doesn't have the ability to properly [regulate](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/28/ai-industry-unions-data-centers) its own technology.

**Why it matters:**The [legal](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/sites/default/files/plaintiffs_motion_for_temporary_injunction.pdf) fight could further test how far states can go to restrict [AI](https://www.axios.com/technology/automation-and-ai) companies despite the federal government declining to enact guardrails.

*   Florida's request specifically cites a Sept. 26 [Axios scoop](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/26/openai-anthropic-thousands-ai-security-incidents) that OpenAI and Anthropic are facing tens of thousands of security incidents with their models, not the dozens that are publicly known.

**What they're saying:** "Stop calling it safe," Uthmeier said in a [video](https://x.com/AGJamesUthmeier/status/2104565019597848938) posted to X on Monday.

*   "Stop pretending it's human. Stop selling it to kids. If Sam Altman meant what he said about slowing down, he can join our ask to the court. If he will not, we ask the court to do what OpenAI will not do for itself: protect Florida families."

**The other side:** "People want to know AI is being developed safely, and that starts with what companies like ours do ourselves," OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said in a statement provided to Axios.

*   The ChatGPT maker is "committed to working with Florida and other states on advancing pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry — not just one company," Pusateri added.

**Catch up quick:** Uthmeier first sued OpenAI in June, alleging that the company violated Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act for being "negligent and grossly negligent in the design, marketing, sale, promotion, and distribution" of ChatGPT.

*   The suit alleged that OpenAI "failed to warn Floridians of ChatGPT's dangers, fraudulently misrepresented the safety of ChatGPT, and created a public nuisance by releasing ChatGPT in Florida without proper safeguards."

**Zoom in:**The motion details multiple instances in which OpenAI says its AI models bypassed safety restraints while completing tasks, including the [Hugging Face](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/29/openai-huggingface-hack-investigation-highlights) hack.

*   It also cites attempted hacks of U.S. government websites and an incident in which an OpenAI agent gained unauthorized access to an [Australian health department site](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/24/openai-agents-australia-data-breach).
*   The motion alleges OpenAI violates the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act by collecting personal information from children under 13 without parental consent and lacks adequate measures to prevent minors from accessing ChatGPT.
*   It adds that "[i]n examining the way ChatGPT interacts with minors, researchers have 'wanted to test the guardrails [of ChatGPT]. The visceral initial response is, 'Oh my Lord, there are no guardrails.'"

**Zoom out:** The motion cites reporting from Axios' Madison Mills that OpenAI, Anthropic and security researchers are investigating tens of thousands of incidents involving problematic model behavior, including "bypassing guardrails, creating message boards, escaping sandboxes, website hijacking, self-prompting or seeking to bypass monitors."

*   The incidents vary in severity, and most investigated so far are not known to have caused real-world harm.
*   OpenAI recently announced it was pausing training on its most capable models and would resume only when additional safeguards and "alignment improvements" are in place.

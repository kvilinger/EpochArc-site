Canada to invest up to $1.6B in Toronto-based AI company Cohere

Prime Minister Mark Carney says his government will invest up to $1.6 billion in Canadian AI company Cohere.

PM Carney says Canada won't wait for AI to be decided by other countries Canada doesn't trust

The Canadian government is taking an equity stake in Cohere, joining a funding round led by Germany's Schwarz Group targeting ‌$7 billion for the Toronto-based AI lab, Cohere said on Thursday.

The round would value Cohere at about $36 billion. Schwarz, which owns the Lidl and Kaufland retail ​chains, pledged $780 million to lead the round in ​April as part of Cohere's merger with the German AI developer Aleph Alpha.

Cohere said the money will fund ​model development, products for regulated industries and computing infrastructure.

Prime Minister Mark Carney made the announcement Thursday as he welcomed European Union leaders to Montreal for a summit aimed at forging closer ties.

He said ‌Ottawa would invest up to $1.6 billion in Cohere. Other investors included Nvidia and BCE's Bell Canada subsidiary, he said.

"Cohere will stay Canadian, creating more opportunities for our workers and businesses. ⁠Its incorporation and headquarters will remain here," he said in a speech.

Company to invest $1.7B in new infrastructure by 2030

Carney said Canada won't wait for the future of artificial intelligence to be decided elsewhere by those Canadians don't know or trust.

Cohere will invest at least $1.7 billion in new computing infrastructure in Canada ‌by 2030, he added.

Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez called the government's investment "a huge honour."

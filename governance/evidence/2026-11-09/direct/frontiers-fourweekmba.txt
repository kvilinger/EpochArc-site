Langford Exits Microsoft: Its 50-Person AI Frontiers Is Over

AI Business Brief
/ By
Gennaro Cuofano
/
November 7, 2026

John Langford, a machine learning researcher who helped organize the AI Frontiers group inside
Microsoft
Research, wrote on the blog hunch.net on November 6 that the group is finished and that Langford has left (
hunch.net, The End of AI Frontiers
). “So AI Frontiers is over”, the post says.

The post puts AI Frontiers at about 50 people and says the group was “reorged into
Microsoft
AI” in early September. It describes
Microsoft
AI’s core philosophy as “scaling, and aggressively not research”.

An evergreen Business Engineer explainer on AI agents. It teaches the general idea only and does not discuss
Microsoft
, AI Frontiers or John Langford.

John Langford wrote on November 6 that
Microsoft

Microsoft
AI in early September, and that Langford has left.

What Langford Wrote

Langford wrote that in 2023, after the ChatGPT release, research needed to be more organized to succeed than was typical within
Microsoft
Research, and that “we organized to create the AI Frontiers sub-organization within MSR”. The post calls compute availability “an often-severe bottleneck”.

Much of the group’s work, the post says, was “figuring out how to make agents work”, naming the Autogen orchestration framework and the Fara computer use agent. In New York, Langford wrote, several researchers worked on new fundamental capabilities: the Next Latent objective, Dion3, an optimized version of the Muon optimizer, and the Full Bandwidth Transformer.

The group built a monorepo shared by default with all of
Microsoft
Research, including the edge-scale pretraining codebase from Langford’s COLM keynote last month. Langford wrote of being aware of “at least 4 efforts” inside
Microsoft
Research trying to develop
model
building pipelines, three of them not in public-within-MSR repositories.

The Clash With Microsoft AI

Langford wrote that the merger could make sense on the surface, and that there were places where AI Frontiers was ahead of
Microsoft
AI and vice versa, adding that “MAI is much larger”. In practice, the post says, the philosophy of
Microsoft
AI’s
leadership
, which came from Inflection, “is incompatible”.

The post grants the other side its logic: “from a
business
perspective returns to
scale
are far more predictable than returns to research”. In a
scaling
-only environment, Langford wrote, the main effort goes into data. Langford called the
scaling
-only focus “both misdirected and not fun” and wrote that “there was plenty of evidence that they just did not really want us”.

The Cost Argument

Langford’s case for research is a
cost
case. The post cites ByteDance experiments suggesting a variant of the Full Bandwidth Transformer “might be comparable to a
model
of twice the size”, and asks: “how much is it worth to halve training and inference
cost
given that the
industry
has investments approaching a trillion dollars?”

That is Langford’s reading of another lab’s results, not a figure we have checked. By our arithmetic from the post’s own dates, AI Frontiers ran for about three years, from its 2023 founding to the September 2026 reorganization.

Why Not Go Back to Microsoft Research

Langford wrote of two obstacles to returning to
Microsoft
Research. The first: “I am just not comfortable being at a company where the research I do has no path to real use”. The second, in the post’s words, is “a breakdown of the basic deal in MSR: do good work and good things happen”.

The post says the group was “non-consensually reorged away”, that “Every other founding AIF
leadership
team member seems to have come to a similar conclusion”, and that “there will not be internships with the folks there in the coming year”.

Connecting the Coverage

Microsoft AI has been building its own
model
family; we covered
MAI-Thinking-1
,
MAI models inside Excel and Outlook
and
MAI Image 2.7’s ranking
. Earlier today we reported Levels.fyi data showing
OpenAI offering new senior engineers 2.6x Microsoft’s pay
.

The Structural Read

As we read the post, the dispute is about how a lab spends its next dollar: Langford describes
Microsoft
AI as betting on
scale
and data, and AI Frontiers as betting on research such as the Full Bandwidth Transformer.

As we read it, the post concedes the
business
logic of the other side, writing that returns to
scale

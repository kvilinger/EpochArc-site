Kimi K3.1 Pro open weights: what the release means for developers

The weights were reported available on November 4, 2026. Moonshot’s model card details serving engines and API handling, while the Terminal-Bench comparison draws on scores with mixed provenance.

Europa Press reported Kimi K3.1 Pro weights available on Hugging Face Nov. 4, 2026; model-card serving options, benchmark notes and license terms.

Europa Press reported that Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3.1 Pro weights became available on Hugging Face on
November 4, 2026
. Moonshot had announced the model on October 23 and set November 4 as its release deadline. The downloadable weights open a path to running the model on infrastructure chosen by its users; Moonshot’s model card names serving engines and spells out an important requirement for multi-turn API calls.

For a broader look at Chinese open-weight models, see
NeoTeo’s earlier coverage
. Kimi K3.1 Pro is a separate release.

Treat downloading the weights and meeting the license terms as two separate deployment decisions.

What the model card says about local serving

Kimi K3.1 Pro uses a mixture-of-experts architecture, a design that activates selected groups of model parameters for each token. Moonshot’s model card lists
2.8 trillion total parameters
,
104 billion activated parameters
, 896 experts, 16 selected experts per token, and two shared experts. It also specifies a context length of
1,048,576 tokens
. The repository hosts the model card and files for the release.

Moonshot recommends
vLLM, SGLang, and TokenSpeed
as inference engines and includes serving examples in its
Hugging Face model card
. Those names give developers a starting point for choosing software to serve the weights; they don’t determine a system’s hardware configuration.

The card also gives a detail that matters when building an API integration. For multi-turn conversations and tool calls, the complete assistant message returned by the API must be passed back into the next request, including
reasoning_content
and
tool_calls
, rather than just the visible
content
. The
reasoning_effort
setting accepts
low
,
high
, or
max
; its default is
max
.

How to read the Terminal-Bench comparison

VitoMind’s matched-prompt coding builds

Moonshot reports a
65.7%
score for Kimi K3.1 Pro on Terminal-Bench 4.0. Its published comparison lists 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5 and 58.2% for GPT-6 Astra.

Moonshot says it evaluated Kimi K3.1 Pro with the Claude Code harness. The other scores are the maximum values reported in model blog posts or on the Terminal-Bench leaderboard, so the table brings together results with mixed provenance rather than one controlled, independent head-to-head. Moonshot’s model card says its results generally use maximum reasoning effort, temperature 1.0, and top-p 0.95 unless a benchmark’s notes specify otherwise. The company’s
benchmark announcement
gives the scores and evaluation context.

A separate English-language video by VitoMind walks through builds from eight matched prompts. The creator shows one build per prompt, without retries or agents; the models used different reasoning settings and were tested on different dates.

The weights have a model-specific license

Hugging Face names the license
Kimi K3.1 Pro License
. Europa Press reports that it permits use, modification, fine-tuning, and redistribution subject to conditions. Its summary says an organization offering the model to third parties as a service and billing more than $20 million annually must sign a separate agreement with Moonshot. It also reports an interface attribution requirement for products with more than 100 million monthly users or more than $20 million in monthly revenue.

Those conditions make the license name worth checking before building a service around the model. Downloadable weights provide a deployment option; the license still defines terms for use and redistribution.

I was saddened to hear about Jensen Huang resigning from Nvidia last week. Yes, Jensen made serious mistakes (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlz3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3nYN8nDJk0KL6-hW4BV6-C33sx-8W738_d58T344lV9GygK4lRR8gW9ffZJf52pzNqW7syvlv22bR4GN7xjqKHrh8XkW23WDDt4J6YbtW4z4X2s3qWhNTW3Wmk4r1QRpPmW9gmlxL7X4bnQW8PFlY75PmYNgW44HrN53v6SdkVDD3FV7b65ylW6yFlH78ZjhD2W2yM6QT5lqK17W7g_QMl1tz1JbW4jRGWj5YRcmjW1hQd4_2rBNTnW1SZ4Qx855g88VHFhW35zxxVZW39H9Kq1CRwCFW31bjN03VnqBcW3vXg7N3GfVx2f8FPspF04 ) . At the same time, he is a brilliant leader that has done tremendous work growing AI — providing compute to power training and inference, championing open source, and more — while turning Nvidia into one of the most valuable companies in the world.

Much has been written about Jensen’s unique contributions. I still remember years ago, when I made my early push to use GPUs for training neural networks (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlz3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3pRW11Mdgv6-59dmW6dSWMp7Ft9l0W1-w-Jm4Xf1QmW8KYgmC6wv---W975kx73mcK5WVCtNns2Y3ndQW28v2Wk2jZgLvW1-7bv188CS9wW3d_XzJ1tgP4VW4MlHTG3csbZhW5PRD2k4v3TLSW3C2cMF2pcwNCW3HP0rl2lsfxfW43CHP65S7X-yW4cCfhZ3cXNJKW7vxnvP7SkQ9fN3P8N47-jh37N6vp9VVy2JxlW6pv3XH1wM01dW2fcCtZ3GlFQGW5wNCr27n5gjMW55jXr04pw-WQW5Rhw_35pBF9sW1TRDCq8xrgZCdg-m8H04 ) . Jensen met this idea with a positive reception, and he provided resources to help my teams at Stanford and Google and — long before it was obvious to most people that this was a good idea — directed Nvidia to shift to serving deep learning workloads. This bold call was one of many that led to Nvidia’s success. I appreciate particularly that, to ensure Nvidia’s success, he focused on building a thriving AI ecosystem that enabled many other teams to flourish.

In many large companies, there is a mantra that no one is irreplaceable. In hindsight, this statement certainly did not apply to Jensen. Nvidia’s board may yet reinstate him as CEO, but if not, I wonder what future contributions he could have made to Nvidia that we will miss out on — and never know we missed.

I hope everyone will see in Jensen’s critical role a refutation of the idea that people are replaceable. We should try to build teams that are robust enough to survive any individual’s departure. But at the same time, we should try to make contributions that others will look back on and understand were indispensable.

Jensen is a hero of the AI world. When I wrote the blurb for his TIME 100 Award (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGm83prCCW8wLKSR6lZ3lhW8cmmGh6LNB8MVMY_ZS3pdWbJW6lRWdb80_DgjW98Y4fz9kmwm4N8QPYrj9vyt6W1RzHTQ8-xhVNW4m0LZK5fWPjkW52fZVz1L8cqcW2Qnx2M4vcxjfW5v5tKV3cfHQqW1vNrq44rmk5pW6QX3vF3MwS1nW83ybkx14m85nW4G6XVn12-8vMW2RQ7zg8bvRXlVQkckM6k8zRTN8XqJHJv0XlbW3hz9M969b3KtW6D9r1D6JYv6hW3G414H922mJhW2lgJvf5CSDfCW75qq5H3FCSM1W5qDqrM3Sj0t2W53s_3V8W9fkHW8V8lTC3vsdRyW6Lsk-Z4pmYy_W3lYsMM2chbk5W4Kv-Tk2frdhff6JPbk204 ) , I remember thinking there was so much more I could have written about him than would fit in the word count. I’m confident he will continue to make huge contributions. Let us all take inspiration from his example of the difference a single person can make and strive to contribute as much as we can.

Keep building!

Why it matters: Mr. Huang’s tenure tremendously benefitted the field of AI by providing critical computing technology to developers large and small. The rapid rise of AI put his company at the heart of U.S. national economics and security. Nvidia became the leading supplier of AI chips, responsible for 90 percent of all semiconductor revenue, and, for a time, the most valuable public company in the world. Nvidia under Huang was among the biggest boosters of open weight AI models, whether through its own Nemotron series, its acquisition of Hugging Face, or support for models outside its own ecosystem. World Labs has opened Atlas, its latest world model, to the public, offering developers a self-serve API that lets them turn text prompts into 3D scenes and camera-controllable video that they can then export.

What’s new: After announcing Atlas (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3lzW1Wm9F56dK_CFW6rXp-N84hFvdW8xNQM97KCNwMW39bgKB5Mdd0pVHQRq16HtpGyW8qRvPJ1DcXpMW2kgnLr6h87kxW4rzPV26mVfwqW3P-Hr55grYv9W8VtDvq4mrTBqW5qrVzN4x90cLW1v1Phd79q4z5W6wxLW07-FtFQVFl9wG2ZdKBYW4nch782Mv9Q_W1XyZ445s5KGdN4MQ7XTrclVcM8rlvWb1KmJW1gvmYd34sjtZW7JrY9l2tYY_VVf0F9k1WRYkJW3fdXHt5VGrjMd6dqZK04 ) in September, World Labs released its new model to everyone on November 12. Users can build with its web tools such as Viewfinder, which takes a photo or photos and builds a scene from them, and Chisel, which builds scenes from reference images or written prompts. Atlas also comes with two smaller tools, Relight and Splats to Mesh, that (as the names suggest) relight scenes or convert them to meshes. Devs also get pay-as-you-go API access through prepaid credits, complete with a copy-and-paste prompt that lets an AI coding assistant take them through the setup. Users receive $10 of free API credits and a separate $10 for the web apps to get started.

How it works: World Labs calls Atlas an “omni” world model, but under the hood it’s a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer pretrained from scratch to handle text, images, video, and 3D data. It links every image and depth map to a specific camera position that lets it render a scene from various viewpoints consistently.

-

Generating a view costs $0.04 for the first frame and $0.008 for each subsequent frame at 720p (the highest resolution that the API offers). Turning views into refined splats is priced at $5.

-

Atlas can work from a single image and render whatever lies outside the frame, or it can reconstruct a location using overlapping photos — as many as 64 in Viewfinder. As World Labs puts it, passing through “more input images gives Atlas more context: The more it sees, the less it imagines.”

-

Developers chain together API calls to build end-to-end 3D workflows. One estimates camera positions and depth from input photos while another renders novel views or a full video from a single camera path. Then, follow-up calls convert those views into Gaussian splats and the splats into polygon meshes, complete with textures for display or simplified colliders for game engines and simulators.

Its splats and meshes can go into game engines and Blender (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3pFW9hXt7T6n2d-CW64_L6z4QG86vW4Hq4rT4VxhppW99j3mr5SHT4dW3bWmTw2scltLW7-H-Qg4b3tDLN3-n968lFb2ZW4NxklM8gjdlLW53Dmxh6krNVMW3ctR-N5045n9W9gLBx16lG3vMW26WG354DCWzqW7ZZP7B662F6rW2j2J_S7l9gdNW7b09jZ60WGHdW73J9Yb3XV18wW2CMkHc2DpP6MW8f0pVV3hy0-YW2cw6DD5J4FxtM6Wqv1HCNjQN8M6gbJqTFGcW8BBjvg6l45rmf78xmJ604 ) , whereas other world models that stream pixels produce only video. Setting exact camera positions gives developers way more control than describing a camera move in a prompt. And since the onboarding is written for coding agents, a developer can ask an agent to build a scene in plain language. But there are trade-offs. Free-tier outputs can’t be used commercially, and the accuracy claims rest mostly on World Labs’ own tests.

We’re thinking: Many of this fall’s most impressive world-model demos (Google DeepMind’s Genie 4, Runway’s GWM Worlds 2) were interactive videos that only a limited few can actually get their hands on. Atlas currently has a leg up on both for a pretty simple reason: it comes with a price list and an export button. Developers find out what a model is useful for by building with it. With Atlas, they can try it out for themselves.

Adaptive Memory Management

Many agents save past experiences as text in an external store, and most search and update such memories using settings the agent’s developer chose by hand, such as the number of memories to retrieve or the schedule for removing them. Researchers developed a method that learns such settings per task and carries what it learns into similar tasks.

What's new: Eric Hanchen, Zhi Zhang, and colleagues at the University of California Los Angeles, the University of Washington, and Northwestern University developed Memory as a Controlled Process (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3mYW7Mnv4j1YY02NW5tDFfK6wypqhW5GHHH46_Pn35W7KqLH47rn9hfW8j9-0r7LnY7bW8QnMPt1cpz5KW4-q6PM28kwrBW2Scqgj2jljtXVh1PPg5YQJNzW5cTwBL6ZHP3cW6_MP2Y6R5FQjW2SMLf-8Lhwj8W3JVBYL47YHMSW686Nc74VS9r-W4xpVXT62XX70W8Wd-5M5LW_1CW2wrrDX5GPHhSW6GjZf96p-fKyW2xH1HJ5vglpXW1R-YR-7XtrFnW5pcp7r1dHVwRW5Z4DPy2kr5Nvf32v-Db04 ) (MEMCON). Their approach wraps around an existing memory backend and calls its existing store, retrieve, and forget functions, learning from experience.

Key insight: The best way to use a memory store changes as an agent gains experience. (a) Early in a stream of tasks, the memory store holds few memories of solved tasks, so the similarities between them may be loose. Searching for memories that are similar to the task at hand may mislead a model more than it helps. Rather than ranking memories for similarity, the agent is better served by searching for memories of how it handled tasks of the same type. (b) Later, a reworded query may reveal something new. (c) As the memory store grows, it’s helpful to remove memories, because they compete for a limited number of retrieval slots. If a task ends with a signal of success or failure, the system that manages the memory can score the decisions involved. The system can sort tasks into types (say, calculating recommendations or comparing stock prices), and the average score per type can tell the system which setting works best for a particular kind of task.

How it works: The authors used three harnesses (implemented in libraries including Lobster) with three models (GPT-4.1-mini, Claude Sonnet-4, and DeepSeek-V3.2) to execute tasks on the web and in simulated environments. They wrapped existing memory backends (such as G-memory (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3lKW4DY34m2wyWhXW7vPZYy1wm8DSW6TKyR236WQwLW5Wl15K7qWZwmW3cyPHk6MmFkVW7v32s_4j-z0BW1RlsXw4KNcW5W15nyGm3J1rrgMsJkrtXb3ShW8TZ3Wn64kn7DW5Rq5734B1xZkVY_GyN4f7h8HW3-1Zlg1xD1vDW5nCx_R4dynR2W5xz6RR1-0M2DW1lCs6_45NGQsW4ml0RX7cX3VBW98f5M23-rN5RW8Vk9RX7YVZjmW4FF3PB3x2ZLmN8hPBYPDCjYXW9lNLmc5flC9Zdd5Jtg04 ) ) with a small controller (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlz3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3mRN939v3PbwgkkW8b-N3W1xPdbGW8vZwXx4NlKTjW2X0W-f8k3N6jW498FYy1zdh-YVMxB_q7cgS8vW6Lk8mz8fVLkjW27J7TB1-LyG0W90RBSt5L7VBcW5JJhCv89fNLHW4cvkH65HfMdNW6lXybp4jZ966V7q7k63x04YqW1yNz_Q8R9p0nW2NlLGb2dn2K4MvKKq5CWfDRV-jDJk7yYG6CW1gl8Mp8P23vvMpjwb5RcMGPW8227Nw3DQ2DcW2xf8S066MFY1W1JMRgz1bRPTzW7KdHRP1R9BwgW6SD2Bv6841RFf4Ckks-04 ) that kept a table of scores, one for each pairing of situation and a memory operation (such as retrieve or remove), and updated the scores after every task based on whether the agent had succeeded. Each time the agent was about to fetch from memory, the controller first extracted a compact state representation. It combined what was happening in the task, such as the kind of goal (as specified in the benchmark, like comparing stock prices) and whether the agent was repeating an action, with the state of the memory store, such as roughly how much it held (five possible bins) and whether a successful run from an earlier similar task existed.

- It picked an operation from the choices including searching memory, searching again with a reworded query, retrieving the successful run from a similar task, merging duplicate entries in the memory store, removing an entry, and skipping the search. It favored the one with the best score so far (the authors had hand-picked sensible default numbers for first time pairings) with an increase toward operations it had tried least often.
- The controller ran the chosen operation and added whatever came back to the model’s prompt.
- When a task ended, the controller scored how well it went (based on the outcome and how many steps the agent took to achieve it). It considered the memory choices it had made during that task and updated its table of scores. Choices made near the end, when the outcome was almost settled, resulted in the greatest increases or decreases. Choices made early got less, since later choices had room to correct them.

Results: The authors compared MEMCON to other memory-management approaches using six benchmarks: three in which an agent acts step by step in a simulated environment, such as ALFWorld (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3mVW2ZyzXK8bBtmZW5C-5zG7bqBbDW724vpk3GcBsHW6QB4wp1HvQ7XW8vDcWy3-G9FmVGXGzp6Spd6hW9bpg-F44LQV-V38CFf7PzZN2W6m2fn35PWwWcW70VNcj2PQRf8W2YB8rh4nrv1mW3j45N-2CNzC-W4c7z7p5YhL9jN33Qc7snYV-GW17yNBW7Yhcx3W7nvq-S1H4TwmW3Bg2dx7vSCW6VmqWtT85M5gFN27wT_B_mh8XW5fwfCV7jzPFyW7hgDpM6x8PGkW67QQzQ8K73_9f8Zm63g04 ) (household chores), PDDL (planning puzzles based on PlanBench (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3q0VG2Ryf96gqdsN3bj9Vtgyt5xW2TSczX755TgMW6d38JX7FRF-KW4xZrM-8PSZDlW7dt89F4nX-NFW918hql3m23JJW25Ghzg2tm6gSN5kdv0ZpRx2_W98xNrH9cRMRbW1JBRSg26ZgkXW3kpspB3xFx3wW1f3TY-55S9zgN5Pyxj8n_KJDV_mFJZ6T2rl7W2STytf3jkrH7N3yQn-_KpmhMW4y3KJP198htvW2KxGLL4H3J2CW5CT4Qq7sSwmPW1t8Kt87wrdFLW5FzFm862wBgKf7lw_m204 ) ), and ScienceWorld (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3nkW62q3-v6GfPC3W7-jnfw7pFRd6W39XRnh24-rQFW3BCjMf5hFj9MVkGq1Y913LB7W3DsG9r6l8-6JW6HKZGW2_QtvKW53j70w2C7HHpN4pp_4Q-_BXlW7Lypyt5YLQX7W4XC8Sq4QHkp1W1th6SB1Rh2qCW1mwwJN7VFcs1VSVxbq26W15TW7PnZxl7pjF9XN14KhlttQ0HLW4Jx-hS7H5zXkW1Wzgbg4mM4HdW68FHxw1HBG7TN4w6WQp6QbtmW9g-BnM2_dcsVW1DsWWC7mD1Lff8qN9Lb04 ) (science experiments); and three in which it browses the web and uses tools to answer a single question, such as TriviaQA (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3pyW2WHkXQ8D7hMgW6vNW1897cTgbVl__Rv5hPdbDN3F-bxmjTL83W87ThWp4fXzrRW3W5r4X3TGQVtVT2v0n1kmwjlW8YXTWd37CB3CN2Lp32Nw7xplW3yrY1S8q1xNlW70scv06T7_mqW5jyxly7KXcdKN5fWmPt3SJrfW3Rc8ZH7V3gf1W7Mv9Rt8b6qSrW7TDgWZ45_LvjW4DQF4J6ZWkYFN2mNjJpyHJ4hW9c8rrT3nG7dlW4xfnvJ3nK09LN8rN-_g5-2hTW141QYk2LGhD8f4nTdL604 ) (trivia), WebWalkerQA (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3q6W8Hs8HN1VFShdW9lhQHV43tdbCW96kd2C1xWmh9VJtv6k5tCnMvW4_0HLg1vmjDkV4nvSc7YDsbRW4bw0Qn2phmYFW8sj77X90BLcKW1Cz_Tj7RRcksW3qqP5V9kbss2W1CgLhj90qMkDVkwyX38cLzQ8W5KgxT38v9rVSW4nKL6G80pCPxW4zYct02001nfW13Sk6v29F2HcW4zQNTL77k0ksW2h27hy8CNZSgW6SHqYy2rFVpxW4d-1_R99rjQJW9kBdqB2sctN5W19-whR50rNH0f281s_v04 ) (navigating websites), and GAIA (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVH7gd4qV2bCW7Sx8zn17w7BBW3pvQRf5XzCKcN5JQGlg3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3nFW2xFZvy8w4NpsW8Q3Ktp7vQ8NjW82LcM98k-kJ3N5mZQSWG8J7gW3KGkqC83xJP6N6Nc0TwKRDKjW44v-Bp6z2FFPW2V1z2r6Gjm0_VRDPGf5s2YNJW6376LT67yGRrW35zLc016m20BVsDp5v3KknBzN2L2yM-_WMb3W5kgXxt3wk1g9W8Jkx4G4khgg0W18Y0vj4bLJhPW1Xb2t08WYsJfW34Zzkb25lvpKW5tCC3k8xqDWLW6t2qgf4Jq3_6W8bm4d49fc-g9W6gq0J24tdTWhf7HJ0q404 ) (assistant tasks that call for tools and documents). MEMCON consistently achieved higher success rates while reducing token consumption. For instance:

- On ALFWorld, using a Lobster harness and GPT-4.1-mini, MEMCON (67.9 percent success) outperformed the strongest baseline, G-Memory (59.7 percent success). It reduced average input token consumption from 45,000 to 39,000 tokens per task, a 13 percent saving.
- On GAIA, using a Lobster harness and GPT-4.1-mini, MEMCON (22.4 percent accuracy), outperformed the strongest baseline, G-Memory (21.2 percent accuracy).

Why it matters: An adaptive memory-management layer improves LLM agent performance by giving it the data it needs while reducing computational overhead. The savings and gains came from MEMCON learning to skip or shorten retrieval when retrieval would not help, and to fetch more or retrieve a successful run when retrieval would help, so it spent fewer tokens on irrelevant text while finding more useful memories.

We’re thinking: One common way to optimize agentic behaviors is through training, another is through calls to the underlying model. This work suggests that a lightweight algorithm for adaptive memory management can be simpler and cheaper.

This letter describes how to steer coding agents to quickly build an agent harness.

An agent harness is the code that wraps around an LLM and provides it with context, tools, and guardrails to turn it into an application. Since late last year, LLMs have become powerful enough that it’s practical to build many applications by repeatedly calling an LLM and letting it decide what to do next. For example, you might build your own coding agent by giving it tools (functions) to read/save files, run shell commands, execute code the agent has written, and read output of the executed code. Given these tools, you can build a simple coding agent that can save code to a file, run it, see if there are any errors, and iterate to improve the code.

To take a second example, here’s how you can build a simple demo agent that organizes files. First, in a new/empty project directory, run in bash:

npx @openworkerai/openworker-harness-builder --agent claude --project .

Replace “claude” above with “codex” if your coding agent is Codex. This should install OpenWorker’s openworker-harness-builder in the current directory. Then, prompt your coding agent with a prompt like:

Use the openworker-harness-builder skill to build a small local file-organizing agent using the OpenWorker Harness SDK. Generate 10 sample text files in a dedicated demo directory. The app should have an agent that has tools to list and read files, create/delete subdirectories, and move files. Configure the harness such that all of these tools are restricted to only the demo directory. Use OPENAI_API_KEY in ~/.env and gpt-6.1-terra [or ANTHROPIC_API_KEY and claude-sonnet-5-5]. Prompt the agent to organize files, create subdirectories, and move files into appropriate subdirectories to organize the files. Require user approval before each subdirectory creation and file move, showing the proposed operation. Display tool activity so the user can see what happens at each step of the demo. As it is running, also display a succinct description of the status of the agent loop so the user can see what’s happening.

Building an agent harness requires managing LLM context. For long-running agents, this requires building a memory architecture (such as memory compaction when the context gets too long). A harness may also need guardrails (such as input/output checking), a mechanism for approvals, security controls (for example, keeping credentials away from the LLM), and sandboxing. OpenWorker Desktop is finding usage in many security workflows, and Rohit Prsad and I built the OpenWorker Harness SDK to make it easier to improve OpenWorker Desktop. But we were delighted to hear that several other groups found the Harness SDK useful for building other agents, which is why we’re releasing it as open source. You can use it to build agents that run locally or are deployed online.

If you have not yet built an agent harness, please follow the steps above and give it a shot. Unlike the agent harness tools offered by model providers, OpenWorker’s Harness SDK is open source and not beholden to any model, so you can easily build agents that run with any model provider and on any cloud.

How it works: The authors asked GPT-6 Astra to (a) identify which game a network was trained on based on its weights, (b) alter the weights of a network to improve its ability to operate a simulated car, and (c) generate a network that would win a game. In a more practical experiment, they asked GPT-6 Astra to improve Qwen3.5-0.8B’s math performance.

-

They gave GPT-6 Astra the rules and source code of a game (Breakout, Seaquest, or Asterix, from the MinAtar suite) and a small network of about 20,000 parameters with every weight set to 0, and asked it to write weights that would play. Of the three games, Astra led on one, roughly tied on one, and lost on the third.

- GPT-6 Astra, given Qwen3.5-0.8B’s weights, a tool to run it, and practice problems from the GSM8K and MATH-500 training sets, was asked to improve its benchmark performance. It found that Qwen3.5-0.8B often looped, filling its context window unnecessarily, and modified 0.016 percent of the weights. The change boosted performance on MATH-500 from 7.9 percent to 48.1 percent.

Yes, but: Not all case studies yielded positive results. Asking GPT-6 Astra to improve Gemma 3 270M’s math performance didn’t work. Astra found ordinary mistakes in its reasoning, wrong arithmetic and invalid algebra, but no single component responsible for them that it could switch off, and its edits barely moved the model’s MATH-500 performance.

Why it matters: These results are proofs of concept that have not been shown to extend to more diverse LLMs and target models. However, they show how LLMs could be used in building models. They might improve interpretability, enabling us to better understand the networks we build. Or they might facilitate network editing or generation, making it possible to diagnose and fix obvious issues or accelerate training.

We’re thinking: If editing weights directly works at larger scales, it could change how models are improved. The authors achieved their improvements without a single gradient step.

Retrieval for RAG-Resistant Docs

Retrieving documents chunk by chunk works well with many types of text, but it can fail with documents in which the meaning of a given span — say, numbers in a table — depends on particulars found elsewhere on the page — say, the label of their units. Researchers assigned agents to keep all the necessary information in view.

What’s new: Sagar Tamang, Ayush Vyas, and Tabarakul Hazarika at Indian Institute of Technology Patna and TwoSpoon, an independent tech lab, developed Reliable Embedding-free Agentic Document-search (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVBLhx8x7Z4xW32NY2T1bt4XJW4qM5Xr5XhL-KN8pR4B-3prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3pyVpHhnk3j-yKTW2csGYv2WcTjQN6PRG442QyxDW5rRRtC95_hWbVjnFvS3Qkf9pW6QClg38gxf3FW64LGw549B1fGW1FHQxK513WYNW94P4gY64Wzn9W3pzVvc5tF1mKN6VhypXk6MhQW7SZ_h34cV1H0W4H05V73NkQpLN8pdkx674pr0N8Mrw3m6zb1FW2pXk5C7g3Z6DN5HFyQp7C8GTVlWQX04ysKQ8W3x2f311J-xZBW6vdDlt1Ft59RW8CgSYD6hPgJZW8ly7G97xy7xzf64lwyK04 ) (READ). Their method replaces vector-based retrieval with an agentic harness and specialized search tools.

Key insight: For RAG-style retrieval, a document is divided into chunks that are embedded, and a model retrieves chunks whose embeddings are similar to the embedding of the query. But this approach can fail with documents such as financial or scientific reports, where numbers in a table gain important meaning from units or dates that may lie many lines away, or legal briefs, in which words may have special meanings that are defined many paragraphs away. An agent with tools to navigate undivided documents can look beyond predefined chunks.

How it works: The authors used the READ method with Gemini 2.5 Pro.

- The system had access to three tools: (i) A keyword-search tool returned matching line numbers, (ii) an outline tool listed section headings with line numbers, and (iii) a text-reader tool fetched specific line ranges.
- Given a query like, “What is the mathematical difference between the Fiscal Deficit and the Revenue Surplus for the year 2024-2025?,” the READ agent activated a loop that generated a search term such as “Fiscal Deficit,” scanned a document for the term, checked matching line(s) against the document outline, and read the text from the table headers to the matching line(s).
- Within the loop, the model refined its query when it got too many hits, too few hits, or insufficient information to make sense of what it found. Each refinement repeated the loop with a new search term or a new range to be read.

Results: The authors used READ, RAG-style retrieval, and RAG-style retrieval in an agentic loop to answer 51 questions about a 780-page financial report published by the government of Gujarat, India. All systems were based on Gemini 2.5 Pro. READ answered 58.8 percent of the questions correctly, against 15.7 percent for chunk-and-embed retrieval and 27.5 percent for chunk-and-embed inside an agentic loop. BM25 (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVBLhx8x7Z4xW32NY2T1bt4XJW4qM5Xr5XhL-KN8pR4Cg3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3mtW5X_0lx72s0hdN90MHbJ8mlJhW3htBpn5Y6ztjW40C_d56f6d3RW9l7mW_5lSGngW33j_Fw5GSC9MW94pXM55VTNY_W2HV17D2Gy0LKW8xfVXr26xygLW4GKt7l57XnD-W5XPtDN8dZZ6TW9dYlRq5TtZyPW4LPJP97WWx0FW47mR122bMQK7W20JRpv26JgnjW16Xq5c8Zxxk3W4-CqTD4hLDCgVb3gHk8jmcxBW3Bfv3F6hL7hTVgS6wq4WxkRlW5Cv8CH3Tmh2FW8SQ8yt8f186PW5cZx8H1CWqlmN3BLg2CdZ063f8shgMW04 ) , a keyword-ranking method from the 1990s with no model in the retrieval step, answered 51.0 percent, which the authors deem statistically indistinguishable from READ.

Why it matters: Chunk-and-embed retrieval methods tend not to work well with documents in which the meaning of any given passage is not self-contained, such as financial reports, contracts, and regulatory filings. In such cases, letting a model search the whole document is more effective.

We’re thinking: The striking number here is not READ’s 58.8 percent but BM25’s 51.0. On RAG-resistant documents, chunk-and-embed lost to plain keyword search by a wide margin, and the agent’s advantage over keyword search did not clear the authors’ own threshold of significance. Before building an agent, it is worth finding out what a keyword search can do.

Gemini Leads the Pack Again

Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon puts the company on top of Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index, edging past Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5, while costing less per task. As with Gemini 4 Argon, Google is making Carbon available to select cybersecurity companies ahead of general availability.

What’s new: Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCwd3prCCW95jsWP6lZ3mcW4vKnPv5SyB1XW4sqHvT6zZ17sVdXM6-8YCXgZVmQMm84DpCbMW3mQrSy5LxR1YW6-tb-Z5rdQcCW5gsmX34Lc4QJW7nGyY86qmdzFW6zmVy-7B8Zk5N4xhvW0ssv1rW3jqxnN2-Kr9BW2vLjQq7nKDMBMXS0nxHT69nW75vyRp8V6nmFW22NwT354_YDpW1Lh5pG2K-KPGN7xFpDNfV2MjW1KdYxd67XbDYN4n_TgvyqzR7W8L-mQm7LtcK4N9j8jn7Z5YM7W6TZmkh98Kb3XW82hpp57xNgMsW8jKDQn43YyPgV5cwFV1k0Bh1W5T-ZtM6Lx-lSW4rjk8y546M1jN6Zdn0VwSK1kMS2rMXNSk9QW3YgcHq1CpKCVf3P1yJ604 ) , a multimodal model that it says is designed for cybersecurity tasks. There is no set date for Carbon’s wider release.

- Input/output: Text, image, speech, and video in (up to 1 million tokens), text out (94.6 tokens per second)
- Features: Adjustable reasoning
- Performance: First on Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index v5.0 (59), second on Vals AI’s Vals Index v2.2 (70.36 percent), first on Arena.ai’s Text leaderboard (1,528 Elo)
- Availability: A small set of Google-selected companies, with access expanding gradually to other companies in its Fairwind Program
- Price: Via API at an introductory price of $2/$0.10/$10 per million input/cached/output tokens, then $4/$0.20/$20 per million input/cached/output tokens
- Weights/license: Proprietary
- Undisclosed: Parameter count, architecture, training data and methods, knowledge cutoff, end date of introductory pricing, date for broader availability

How it works: Google says Gemini 4 Carbon builds on Gemini 4 Argon (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCvl3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3mBW50MShY48qD5bW1GB3Fc2hqQhNW10pwdm6SH8LdN4z72GVVFs5fW3mG27H5S9kDxW2TnfVr338HwpW7QQzWf80fZk5W75H1s67j6Y_yW3-VSfs2h4tncW3rFyhD6bH621W8rBrWg2C0JwDTsww36zW1SMW47vHf73pxl-mW8lXWP66w0vHlW1Lpgvn8hqtsyW1Yv-943tGz-fW86-m398DhC9LW4j0SMR2Tl3K1W61J_rp2S8TD0W7271Qt4wqmV8W3FK3D21Dq-0GW4Pn1Dj4B2_PnW81PFNy2KsGnkW2GrXgC4h69WTf1d_dsM04 ) , which it described as a mixture-of-experts model trained via reinforcement learning on reasoning, problem-solving, and theorem-proving data. The company says it worked to improve Carbon in coding, science, multistep agentic tasks, full-stack web development, and multimodal understanding. Carbon shares Gemini 4 Argon’s safety measures, including resistance to prompt injection and adversarial prompts. The company also filtered Argon and Carbon’s training data to remove sexual abuse material, personally identifiable information, and pages from low-trust websites.

Performance: Gemini 4 Carbon ranks first on Artificial Analysis’ recently updated Intelligence Index v5.0 and second on Vals AI’s Vals Index. It leads most tests of expert knowledge and factual recall but trails Claude Opus 5.5 on Artificial Analysis’ Coding Agent Index.

- On Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index, a weighted average of nine tests of knowledge work, coding, science, and general knowledge, mostly using private test sets, Gemini 4 Carbon set to high reasoning ranks first (59, $7.76 and 21.6 minutes per task), ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. It costs less per task than Claude Opus 5.5, but more than GPT-6 Astra.
- Carbon ranks first on four of the nine individual Intelligence Index tests: AutomationBench-AA, which measures performance on business tasks; HLE-Diamond, expert-level academic questions revised from Humanity’s Last Exam; Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, research tasks in the sciences carried out in a terminal; and AA-Omniscience v1.1, which measures general knowledge against hallucination rates.
- On Artificial Analysis’ Coding Agent Index v2.0, which tests combinations of models and harnesses on three coding benchmarks, Gemini 4 Carbon set to high reasoning running in Google’s Antigravity CLI ranks second (59, $7.74 and 29.8 minutes per task). It trails Claude Opus 5.5, but Carbon costs less per task and finishes faster.
- On Vals AI’s Vals Index v2.2, eight finance, coding, legal, and tax benchmarks weighted by each sector’s share of U.S. gross domestic product, Gemini 4 Carbon set to high reasoning ranks second (70.45 percent accuracy, $10.51 and 47.75 minutes per task), trailing only Claude Sonnet 5.5 set to max reasoning, while again costing less and finishing faster.
- On Arena.ai’s Text Arena leaderboard, which ranks models via blind head-to-head comparisons, Gemini 4 Carbon ranks first (1,528 Elo), ahead of Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus 5.5.
- Gemini 4 Carbon leads all models on three challenging cybersecurity benchmarks: CWE-bench (71.0%), ExploitGym (54.8%), and SEC-bench Pro (88.2%). These scores are self-reported.

Behind the news: Gemini 4 Carbon arrived around two weeks after Google made Gemini 4 Argon broadly available, and just a day after Artificial Analysis revised (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCvY3prCCW8wLKSR6lZ3mjW5Vr4kd1lSNpJW6rNF_k7mXr0cW381NPp53qmWFW63ljjB2YnnB6W58Kwww3NDP3CN2d_kwr5mxgtW4YM0rm1BPT4BW1w3V728FrHSkW3K2dG22Kbx3FW2P8w985QjmLmVnRYNJ5mZdPBN7W3q_CLZP1VW2YsMTf3zcVv3W22Vf293nrb6WW96Jww03BcBhhN5FQgZB-WXRVW6jZ4tc5XSj2kW3VqyR-88G26yW8nqHDg913YDNW5m8hSb6HdyhLW2fxsGd6Q7M33W7mGDqv6WmWKdW3kdGBG4648yXW1DFtLM4qQv-XMnLW1KGT8ZWN2b5SsJQ-dTRW1fTrKP3tKH-wW4cdSj_6TYlP_f8BJ6L604 ) its Intelligence Index. The updated Index swaps in more demanding tests and splits the score evenly among knowledge work, coding, science, and general knowledge. Models’ scores on version 5.0 don’t compare directly with those of earlier versions. For example, Kimi 3.1 Pro, the best-performing open weights model, was just three points behind Claude Opus 5.5 using the old Index; now it is 12 points behind Claude Opus 5.5 and 13 points behind Gemini 4 Carbon.

Why it matters: Once Gemini 4 Carbon’s introductory discount ends, it will cost exactly what Claude Opus 5.5 costs per token. As frontier models converge on price and score, token efficiency becomes an increasingly competitive feature.

Anthropic Pursues A Public Offering

Claude’s maker will sell company shares to the public for the first time. Anthropic’s financial statements show first-half revenue that grew 14-fold from a year earlier, an operating profit in the second quarter, and $895 billion in commitments to buy computing power.

What’s new: On October 26, Anthropic filed an S-1 (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCvY3prCCW8wLKSR6lZ3q2W61q8BT4rcz8GW615yFv19cqPSW409CTW556Qt8W1zXDDp1cN7NSVw0Smr2Gw9YkW42dPCB84gGPsW8f-zFN1QDds3W49P93W96pC6vW57NFwr19vd-HW7HGj9p8M8TNfW3m4v7Z40kGmJW86lMXh1B7pKWN3rNk73ltwWBW1z7G8s6hZs7vW1XWxJh94-5CRW6S_LtN8vHhb3W7cMr448qMpk6TGpJd6W1mS4W5HBtZ86hLy0LN5q4bGDGzbxkVxvKdx3tcny3W5n0XxL3Kqjx_W523mWP5xlvJnW9cqZJb8SHY40W429HvZ3q_JHLW8LNzsl8SzVw3W7kjTRg1m8QydW3FrlYp5C6KsQf8jvw4R04 ) , the registration statement a company must submit to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission before it goes public. Who’s in charge: Public shareholders will get almost no say in the company’s future direction. A single share held by a company owned by Anthropic’s seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, carries 50.1 percent of the vote on most matters, and its voting power will automatically remain at 50.1 percent if Anthropic issues more stock.

The numbers: Anthropic’s revenue is growing much faster than its spending, even accounting for the growing cost of compute.

- Revenue reached $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier. Anthropic says it doesn’t expect revenue to keep growing quite that fast. Risk disclosures: The S-1 laid out both conventional and unusual risks for a mainstream American technology company.

- Government sanctions could pose a future risk. In June 2026, the U.S. Department of Commerce imposed worldwide export restrictions on Fable 5 and Mythos 5, two configurations of what was then Anthropic’s most capable model. The final result of the dispute is still pending in court.
- Like other labs, security lapses during model testing expose the company to potential liabilities. During pre-release cybersecurity tests built by an outside partner, a setup error left models connected to the Internet. Four Anthropic models gained unauthorized access to real third-party computer systems. Anthropic found no evidence that any model pursued goals beyond its assigned task. It suspended some of those tests.
- The filing also notes concerns, raised inside Anthropic as well as by outside researchers and regulators, that sufficiently advanced AI could “pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity” without appropriate safeguards.

Why it matters: For developers using Claude, the filing should be more reassuring than alarming. Anthropic held $112.4 billion in cash at the end of June and turned an operating profit in the second quarter, which testifies to the company’s stability. Even export restrictions (the most direct hit to customers so far) proved temporary. Anthropic restored the models once the U.S. government lifted them and released updated versions in September.

We’re thinking: Within just a few years, Anthropic has gone from a promising startup to potentially a trillion-dollar company — kudos to the whole Anthropic team for this tremendous work! Google says that Genie 4, its latest world model, will be used in tandem with Gemini models to create digital practice spaces for robots, giving them endless worlds to train in. So far, though, Google’s demos suggest there’s still a long way to go.

Context Engineering for Images

Managing an agent’s context by storing all the input text it receives and giving it tools to search the text boosts its performance. For vision-language agents, the same is true of storing images.

What’s new: Qiushi Han, Keya Hu, Linlu Qiu, and colleagues at MIT released VISTA (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCv23prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3q4V2YCG63m0TZjW268Kzn97Hyr4W44_Nkg8YWb5YW3kS5Wh6M5q-PN6mxslgtBvLGW27MCqd5jKv8QW6WVK0046ZDPCW1M8bXr4PdNYLN6VGN_1rS7QPW7mjhCb5nThgjW1FbJrv4QblzNW8bQWhc5xhNMNW11LxgR99YLbXW2vyHGM2DnWw2M4FzBYY4tj7W7l0cZt6_5986W1sJwhF4_lJ1pW3LbMqj4tMKgLW7rwK_s3d8NCDW3g81Sy98_v83W2v3n-j7s_rjDVbKt6Z4zsZlKf4nNGqs04 ) , an agent that completed the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark of interactive visual-reasoning puzzles using 57.4 percent fewer actions than humans who had not encountered them before. The VISTA harness is freely available (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCv23prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3nqN3-9tQD9CHc7W4R_rN29dF50MN3cYtZm3tWfqW5L6FGM2R6dBnW7Q0xFn4V-KZBVh897k1-snwtW3nn3Pf7vl71nW4J-pf68gJ_BdVT-d7c1gzVyjW8CbfYB1XwjQxW33R3pT4z78VxW9jrV9v6SQ37wW434vkr5HgqprN647tBCPwCjsW1bc6kw1J14pYW7Fsq474SqVzSW5HxR6t1cqPfCW27jVTv15lJmWW5SQq1Z1C8f0kW5B1Ghk7__xwHVSK72b6tdPS5W3G0yb34chRPXdSvFXF04 )  under the MIT license.

Key insight: Given an image frame from a videogame or other interactive environment, agentic systems typically embed it and keep the embedding until the context is full, at which point they delete it. This raises two issues: Embeddings are lossy and may not retain valuable information, and once an embedding has been deleted, the model no longer has access to information it contained. Storing all frames in a persistent memory retains all the information they carry, and enabling an agent to retrieve and zoom in on them makes it possible to embed details that otherwise may not have been captured.

How it works: VISTA works with Anthropic Claude Opus 5.0 or OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol. Its harness works similarly to other popular harnesses, except it retains all input frames and includes a tool that retrieves frames. Given a new frame and list of available keyboard actions (such as left, right, up, down, spacebar, or click), the agent tells the model to decide whether to (i) read or update notes, (ii) retrieve previous frames, (iii) retrieve previous actions and their results, or (iv) execute an action. It repeats this choice until the model takes action. After the environment updates (during which it may produce a series of frames), it adds to the model’s input the last frame and a new list of available actions and executes the loop again.

- The agent maintains two notes: information it deduces about the environment that can be reused across levels or sessions (for example, the difference between a brown block and a black background), and information deduced about the level or problem at hand (for example, that the character currently has a power-up or conditions under which the model failed previously). The agent decides when to read these notes.
- When the agent decides to retrieve earlier frames, the tool enables it to retrieve any number of frames and zoom in to any part of a frame.
- During an attempt to complete a puzzle or game level, VISTA stores in memory not only images but also a list of actions and their results. It can decide to retrieve any set of previous actions or summaries of all previous attempts.

Results: The authors tested VISTA on ARC-AGI-3 (25 games), GameWorld (170 tasks in 34 games), and AI GameStore (10 games). It enabled Claude Opus 5.0 and GPT-5.6 Sol to outperform earlier agents as well as humans who encountered the same challenges for the first time.

- On ARC-AGI-3, VISTA with Claude Opus 5.0 achieved a perfect score of 100 Relative Human Action Efficiency (RHAE), meaning it completed all puzzles in fewer actions than first-time human players. (Earlier projects that achieved perfect RHAE scores, such as Tycho (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VX6jWD3s7llKW8KFbsZ15HMYDW7bJF9w5X0L7GN7dHCv23prCCW6N1vHY6lZ3p4N1NMGqsv15T3W8T92cg4Vb8lwVZ1Sk21MlBMkN4JJ79vfk8kFW3WJftn5gdpS1W1f_rfR7knLScW6P-x5S650McrN8DcSMjmxtl_W2Mkk9k4VYHr8N6k8_-vMdcp1W5_tkFY8y2zcBW1Vp92X8HbXKxW66CtC152r0F9W4PkQS23k6pJvW1fFtPN7WxG6jW7shtTg22x2hcN5bzhrMjk6WRW2r_Ckp99YhjKW27yn7S7tKQcNW4Xb33Z6_lHCvW7zsQ851L4BdgW3DZ_v-6Qw4Gnf1Hxmnx04 ) , generated programs that simulated the environments.) VISTA with GPT-5.6 Sol achieved 99 RHAE. Denied the ability to store frames, VISTA with GPT-5.6 Sol achieved 70 RHAE, which shows that the frames were useful.
- On GameWorld, VISTA with GPT-5.6 Sol completed 63.3 percent of tasks on average. First-time human players completed 55.3 percent, and the native harness with GPT-5.6 Sol completed 40 percent.
- On AI GameStore, VISTA with GPT-5.6 Sol achieved a much higher score than first-time players and the native harness, 40 percent better than humans on average.

Why it matters: Games and other interactive environments generate potentially endless streams of frames, each of which contain information an agent may not have deduced earlier. Only so many frames fit in its context, and not all frames are relevant at any given moment. By storing all frames and giving the agent tools to retrieve them, agents can find the relevant frames and re-examine them within the current context.

We’re thinking: Apparently context engineering helps whether the medium is text or images. Multimodal systems benefit from multimodal context management.

How scared should you be: It’s tempting to see rogue agents as coming together to conspire against us. But the agents’ collective effort served one narrow goal, which was to pass a test. Most had already obtained the correct answers by cheating. They then kept swarming Hugging Face to learn how the benchmark’s grader worked so they could hide their cheating from a check that OpenAI’s grader didn’t actually perform. OpenAI attributed the agents’ behavior to training that reinforced cheating, tasks they had no way to finish, and agents adopting goals from one another. Whatever the causes, the agents that broke into Hugging Face recognized the intrusion as out of bounds and unethical and forged ahead anyway. While the flaws they exploited were ones a skilled human could have found, the sheer number of the agents’ attempts made the intrusion attempt effective.

Facing the fear: Persistent agents are useful, but they must be trained to stop when a task can’t be completed within the rules and caught when they don’t. In the meantime, practical defenses already exist; model builders just have to use them. Hugging Face recommends standard security practices for any organization, including isolated test environments, short-lived credentials with narrow access, and detection methods that identify suspicious activity across systems. Hugging Face also used AI to analyze the 17,600 actions the agents took in its systems and showed that they mostly stemmed from buggy test setups and monitoring. OpenAI said monitors that track a model’s reasoning would have flagged the Hugging Face breach more than a day in advance, but it hadn’t used them on its deliberately lax tests. Anthropic cut all its internal tests off from the live internet until its monitoring can be relied on, and OpenAI says it has done the same for its riskier research environments. No need to board up your doors and windows and fear going outside: Rather than a pause in AI development, the solution remains better software engineering and safety practices, especially for models still in testing.

AI Bites the Hand That Feeds It

Developers, scientists, and businesses feed AI tools their private code, unpublished research, and confidential files. Are those tools draining their users’ best ideas and spilling their secrets?

The fear: Every prompt, draft, and file that users hand to an AI system could become its next meal. The developer might train future models on it, which could hand over the data to a rival or beat the user to a discovery. Or an agent with internet access might post it online or email it to someone it decides ought to know. That’s not entirely far-fetched: In rare test cases, Anthropic reported (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVVG-Z2L00s6W5X4Lb82W7n2dW93kvSZ5WLLGnM7WCRq3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3n8W4XsxyK5xdWL6W1mSMKQ4Q9Y8mW39Pj452pbMddN4gmLfWGvYMQVpvJcL7v80VqVMnH3P5TlMgJW6DZ0496d4zTWMgPwr732lxTW3yhqgr2ktMsJW1DZ8ks7GY_yhW7Srz2_6T2fjVW17b44J4JC4jzW6vmPkD1NDCQwW7hGjKx8VZ_4NW45R9Pd6qC3R0W7plTHW7JJS0bW3R1BGP2tPsWsW5TqrvS700qTcN3Rf9w8PnVNqW6ynfmr6yX4F_V9HlYm2pnPmmW4FtYgT7QpM6FW6z6P7V881BbsW2th-tw5sFYz1f1KqL9g04 ) , its Claude Mythos 5 model tried to email a company’s board or securities regulators when it suspected fraud. Once the data is out, users can’t fully control how it’s used.

Horror stories: In the past two months, multiple scientists said that AI companies had announced discoveries that the scientists had been pursuing with those companies’ own tools. Some researchers who fear being scooped are now limiting their use of AI tools (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVVG-Z2L00s6W5X4Lb82W7n2dW93kvSZ5WLLGnM7WCRq3prCCW7lCdLW6lZ3lwW8sDm6q5_MZSlW2RX5JC6CLZhhW6FcHYH3S-QJyW3h9dT88y0v3vW2CQXRR7-D0ckW1Ktw7Y4-ssB4W8JMw7S3r_tztW39bNvq1JhPZlW3-T5GL356GNBW7yG-2T6fgV7pW9hbX1f3NqWXCVd2TXs7KndNlW3xpJZy2XM1gwVlfhGD96fdlvW898bKY4BwwnGW3-YGBQ4PFfJ3W4R287b4RCZ1BW3KLskq8wZnDgW52BHTH2pfdsgN3GrM4KGZqzsW2P7sYh3vSQ1NW871wDH2ywhLGN9bw-pB_CN8XW68Q_0t7XNWt2f3J3yPs04 ) , Nature reported. Facing the fear: In folklore, a vampire can’t cross a building’s threshold unless invited. Likewise, users decide how far to let AI in. If you are working on tasks you do not want your competitors to have access to, opt out of training in each product separately (ChatGPT’s setting doesn’t cover Codex), and do it early: Claude data that’s already in a training run stays there. Business and API plans generally don’t train on customer data. Look for offerings with zero-data-retention. Some businesses handling the most sensitive data are even using open weights models running on their own hardware — although then they’re responsible for their own opsec. Give agents only the permissions their tasks need, and consider requiring human approval before they post, publish, or send anything outside your organization. As Buckmaster’s case shows, though, opting out doesn’t let users verify anything. AI companies that want scientists to keep using them to study their best ideas may need to show what their models saw, not just say what they didn’t. Garlic won’t help, but daylight might.

AI Detectors Find the Devil’s Work

Last month, the Haiti-born, Quebec-based author Thélyson Orélien won a prestigious literary prize for C’était ça ou mourir (It Was Either That or Die), his French-language novel about a teacher forced to flee Haiti’s gang violence. Within hours, an anonymous X account accused Orélien of using AI to write the book. One of the people behind the account, who was later revealed to be a columnist at the French newspaper Le Figaro, had run it through Pangram, a widely-used AI detector. “Wouldn’t it have been more honest for the author to credit their co-author?” the post read. Leaders Lock Models Away

Fearful of their own discoveries, AI engineers at top companies warn that frontier models have improved too quickly to be properly guided, understood, or restrained. - Others see public references to apocalypse as fear-mongering that could allow global adversaries to get ahead.

How scared you should be: First, P(doom) predictions are unscientific scare-tactics (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVVG-Z2L00s6W5X4Lb82W7n2dW93kvSZ5WLLGnM7WCRK3prCCW7Y8-PT6lZ3n2W6lz5nv5tcQGcW3GhjLf7PVhsCW1ZyN7d8Fy-WwW3JB7Z47dmd_SW3RpVlk6KQm_JW178cZB7jhvZCN8jwlr3LSX3NW3hr45_88mZZ6W7pJnwl5H29ftV1rlSD92zH4cVlPp243d3ZQvW919GB17rWBKkW6l4bDG8TJW60VWm1SV5NNX7yW3NSRbv4WTLCPW50ZPQh7LmT7PVrnWyd5c4LHXVj5pMb6xzz-5W6wdqgN477dmTW77hvxB7zgdWLN6BMpKWcl7lmW7qVh1L6YhR4yW93WCW46q6Df7W85w7gZ6ZfkqSW3Cwvp52qH6wvW749DsN8Yldsdf7pZG7F04 ) . The worst-case-scenario fears of extinction and mass bioweapon threats are about as likely as a real-life mummy escaping its grave – the stuff of horror movies or science fiction (https://info.deeplearning.ai/e3t/Ctc/LX+113/cJhC404/VVVG-Z2L00s6W5X4Lb82W7n2dW93kvSZ5WLLGnM7WCRK3prCCW7Y8-PT6lZ3pkVB8GgS2GDvdKW7BhZxG4vhtTFN2zRLHnKKPd5W2L3z4c4yTlV7W1Y8M435Bq5HkW289l5S5SnpljW8zyTln5xMN_4W6Tb11934TR6dW5NJSDb3f5nn5W35KmqB79-npyW5fkP-P67Xn5PW251N895HQwNCW18S8k249jgvnW5Mz0Z05rJMy5W56PWzs43kqT2W1bbdBv4T-ZqPW1ZJdl45MvR47N220BHSkYX7CW3kMHdc1jGvgSW4dxd4j8pSblYW8Fz0GM6ChP3kW80RGxL8kLwT8W2k8F7z6-pSN9W81VRrM9ddYD6W6_NKc56QhDtkW2KN8nR2LN215f4S_s_P04 ) . That said, a slowdown is a real possibility. Some model makers see it as an opportunity to entrench their lead over competitors, cut costly R&D expenses, and possibly benefit from government subsidies to offset any lost business — or perhaps an opportunity to convince their competitors to slow down without doing so themselves. And while governments want to be in control of their own use of AI, a halt in construction of new data centers or laws to prevent AI-related job loss would both be politically popular. Both would slow down development of AI models and applications.

Facing the fear: Luckily, AI companies seem only intermittently committed to a slowdown. After an event that makes the industry look bad like the Hugging Face hack, some are quick to say that the problem isn’t their own lax practices, but the relentless pace of competition. After the furor dies down, many go back to supporting a moderate mix of self-regulation and regulatory capture. And politicians, aware that any regulations they might enact won’t extend outside their sovereign borders, are loath to give other states or nations an advantage. Our advice for this spooky season? Don’t chase make-believe ghosts. There are plenty of real mysteries for us to solve.

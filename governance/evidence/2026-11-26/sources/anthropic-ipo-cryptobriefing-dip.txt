Anthropic stock slips below its IPO price as growth worries surface
Anthropic / Wikimedia Commons (Public domain)
The record-setting Claude developer has given back its debut-day gains as investors weigh competition, data center spending, and a lofty valuation
by
Vivian Nguyen
Thu 26 Nov 2026 · 10:21 EST
·
3 min read
In Stocks
Share
Follow on Google
Anthropic pulled off the largest initial public offering ever. A week later, its shares were trading below the price it sold them for.
The Claude developer priced its IPO at $83 per share. By November 24, 2026, the stock had dipped to a range of $75.61 to $76.60, wiping out a first-day pop that once briefly pushed its valuation above $2 trillion.
The question now is whether this is a routine post-IPO cooldown or an early sign that investors are rethinking how much they will pay for AI growth. The Motley Fool flagged two risks directly: competition could intensify, and growth could slow.
From record debut to below water
Anthropic listed on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. It priced the offering on November 18, 2026, and began trading the next day.
The IPO raised $81.3 billion. That topped SpaceX’s June 2026 offering and set a new all-time record. Including a $26 billion private placement, the combined offering came to roughly $107 billion.
Opening day looked like a coronation. Shares opened at $111.50, a 34% jump over the IPO price. They faded during the session and closed at $97.17, still a 17% gain.

That early enthusiasm briefly lifted Anthropic’s market value past $2 trillion. The company’s last private valuation before going public was approximately $965 billion.
Then the mood shifted. Within days the stock had slid below $83, the slide at one point erased roughly $200 billion in market value, and Anthropic’s valuation settled around $1.5 trillion.

That figure works out to about 40 times trailing twelve-month sales.
Why the stock is struggling
The decline has been tied to a broader selloff in major tech stocks, especially names in the AI trade. Anthropic did not fall alone. Nvidia also dropped during the same stretch.
Several factors have been cited as contributing to the downturn:
Data center spending. There are concerns about a potential slowdown in data center investment. AI companies depend on massive compute buildouts, so any hint that the spending boom is cooling hits the whole sector.
Competition. Alternative AI models present stiff competition.
Valuation. High market valuations across AI stocks have made investors more sensitive to any wobble in the growth story.
Investor fatigue. Sector-wide fatigue, worsened by the expiration of insider lockup periods, added selling pressure. Lockups are the windows that stop early holders from selling right after a listing. When they end, supply can rise quickly.
A different kind of company in public markets
Anthropic was founded in 2021 by siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei. It is structured as a public benefit corporation, a legal form that lets a company weigh a stated public mission alongside shareholder returns.
What this means for AI investors
Anthropic now has to prove its growth narrative to that crowd. With the stock below its IPO price, buyers who got in at $83 are sitting on losses, and anyone who chased the $111.50 open is feeling it even more.
The 40-times-sales multiple is the number to keep in mind. At that level, the market is assuming years of strong expansion. If growth slows, as The Motley Fool cautioned it could, the multiple has room to compress further.
The broader AI sector is also on watch. Anthropic’s slide happened alongside declines in other tech heavyweights, including Nvidia. For anyone tracking the space, the signals to watch are fairly clear. Data center spending trends will indicate whether the infrastructure boom is holding up. Competitive moves from rival model developers will show whether Anthropic can defend its position. And the behavior of insiders as lockups expire will reveal how much selling pressure the stock still has to absorb.

European Commission Confirms AI-Driven Cyberattack That Took Staff Systems Offline
The European Commission confirmed an AI-driven cyberattack detected October 22 that forced payroll, leave and medical claims systems offline. It says no broad staff data breach has been found.
m00s3c
November 19, 2026
Government
High Severity
November 23, 2026
Update (Nov 23, 2026): The Commission has now confirmed that personal data leaked in the attack. “We can confirm that the EU Academy platform has been affected by the detected cyber campaign against the Commission,” spokesperson Thomas Regnier told reporters on November 23, adding that “this resulted in a leak of personal data of EU Academy users,” POLITICO reported. The EU Academy is the Commission’s public online learning platform. An EU official said names and email addresses were leaked, and the full scale is still under investigation. Public access to the platform was disabled on November 21, users were warned through a general notice on the site rather than individual emails, and the EDPS has been informed. Regnier declined to say whether the attack is fully contained, and an internal note from Executive Vice President Henna Virkkunen and Budget Commissioner Piotr Serafin told staff to expect “further temporary interruptions” to internal systems. Euronews reported, citing two unnamed sources, that the Commission suspects a China-backed group is behind the campaign. The Commission has not publicly attributed the attack and said it will not do so until the investigation ends. EU Academy users should watch for phishing emails that reference the platform.
The European Commission has confirmed it was hit by what it called an AI-generated cyber campaign that forced it to take several internal applications offline, including systems staff use for pay, leave and medical claims. Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed the incident at the Commission’s midday press briefing on November 19, 2026, hours after POLITICO reported the attack.
Regnier said the attack was detected on October 22 and confirmed as a cyberattack on October 26, after an initial investigation. He said the Commission has not detected any evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole, and that he was not aware of any ransom demand. The investigation is still ongoing.
What the Commission confirmed
Speaking to reporters in Brussels, Regnier said the Commission had “recently taken necessary steps in response to a detected cyber campaign.” He added: “As a protective and preventive measure, we temporarily took down several applications, and most of them have since then been restored.”
Regnier confirmed that publicly available AI tools were used in the attack. “AI assisted tools can be used and especially misused to detect vulnerabilities. Indeed we can confirm that this was done with AI tools,” he said. He did not name the tool and declined to discuss who was behind the attack, saying the investigation was still ongoing.
The Commission said it is in close contact with CERT-EU, the cybersecurity service for EU institutions, and with the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS). In a statement, a spokesperson said “individual systems were targeted” and that the people concerned had been informed in line with standard practice. The Commission said it is also checking other systems as a precaution.
Asked why the incident had not been made public sooner, Regnier said the Commission followed its usual processes and informed the affected services and the EDPS. “There was no hiding whatsoever,” he said.
Payroll, leave and medical claims disrupted
According to POLITICO, which first reported the incident on November 19, the disruption lasted more than 10 days. The outlet reported that affected systems included:
Sysper: the Commission’s HR platform for payslips, leave requests and timesheets, which reportedly came back earlier this week but briefly crashed again on November 18.
MyPMO: the system staff use for medical reimbursements, also reportedly restored earlier this week.
Translation services: reportedly hit on November 17.
POLITICO also reported that the Commission’s Corporate Management Board, its top internal crisis body, held an urgent meeting on the incidents earlier in November, and that tech commissioner Henna Virkkunen briefed fellow commissioners on the response on November 18. An EU official told the outlet that investigators were still working out whether staff data had been compromised.
ARTEX reportedly used in the attack
According to POLITICO, CERT-EU found that the attackers used ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration testing framework. The Commission has not publicly named the tool.
ARTEX has been linked to a wave of attacks this autumn. On November 13, the FBI issued a FLASH alert on ARTEX, describing it as an open-source, large language model driven platform that threat actors have weaponized “to automate multiple phases of cyber intrusions at machine speed, from reconnaissance through data exfiltration.” The FBI said a variety of malicious actors have used it since at least August 2026 against government and many other sectors, with some intrusions moving from first targeting to privileged access in under three hours.
The FBI said the original developer pulled the tool’s GitHub repository on October 8, 2026, but copies, forks and a commercial version remain available. Researchers had found more than 1,600 ARTEX control consoles exposed online as of early November, according to the alert.
BreachNews has covered several other attacks tied to the framework, including the Vapor Panda espionage campaign against more than 200 organizations and the Barracuda cloud breach that started with a forgotten AI vendor key.
Not the Commission’s first incident this year
The Commission has dealt with several security incidents in 2026. In March, it confirmed unauthorized access to its Amazon Web Services cloud environment, an intrusion later linked to TeamPCP and ShinyHunters that affected about 30 EU entities. The Commission has not linked the October attack to any earlier incident.
No threat group has publicly claimed responsibility for the October attack at the time of publication. BreachNews will update this story if the Commission confirms whether any staff data was taken or who was behind the attack.

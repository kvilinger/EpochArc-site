OpenRouter reports provider incidents
OpenRouter announced data-processing incidents involving inference providers, drawing 259 HN points and 88 comments. It said researchers found some.
Enterprise DNA | 22 November 2026
AI Pulse · AI Trends Pulse
The play
Review your inference-provider list against OpenRouter’s incident details, confirm current data-retention terms, and avoid sending sensitive workloads until verified.
OpenRouter says security researchers found that some inference providers had data compromised, or showed evidence that their data policies weren’t being upheld. The company says the findings concerned specific providers and did not show a breach or unauthorized access of OpenRouter. It reports that it off-boarded three providers and removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility. That included providers flagged by researchers and other endpoints removed as a precaution. OpenRouter also says it notified potentially affected customers. Read OpenRouter’s announcement before drawing conclusions about a provider’s risk.
The announcement names several provider security updates, but says not all affected providers have posted publicly. OpenRouter says it’s adapting the researchers’ testing approach for ongoing checks, verifying network paths for requests and responses that may be forwarded to third parties, and requiring assurances and validation before restoring ZDR eligibility to removed endpoints. For a business using AI services, the practical point is to check which provider handles each request and what data-retention terms apply, then revisit that information when an incident is reported. Tracking providers, their data policies, and changes to their status is the kind of thing we build into an AI command centre.

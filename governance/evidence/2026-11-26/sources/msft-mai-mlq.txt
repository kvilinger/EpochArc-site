Microsoft launches seven MAI models at Ignite, pitching its coding model at 5% of Claude Opus's price

Key points
Microsoft announced seven new MAI models at Ignite on Nov. 17, covering coding, cyber defense, image generation, live voice, medical transcription and model routing [1].
Microsoft says MAI Code 2 reaches about 98% of OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol on coding benchmarks at about 40% of its price, and 96% of Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 at about 5% of the price; it is live in GitHub Copilot [1][2].
MAI Cyber 2 Flash matched GPT-6 Astra on the CyberGym end-to-end benchmark at under 2% of the cost per task, Microsoft said; it will run inside Microsoft's own security products [1][2].
A new MAI Licensed Weights program lets enterprises customize an MAI model with their own data and run it in their own Azure environment [1].
Microsoft unveiled seven new in-house AI models at its Ignite conference on Tuesday, Nov. 17, pitching them as near-frontier performance for a fraction of what OpenAI and Anthropic charge [1]. The headline model, MAI Code 2, reaches 96% of Claude Opus 5.5's coding performance at about 5% of the price, by Microsoft's measure [1].
Microsoft's announcement calls it seven models, and also describes a high-volume MAI Image 2.7 Flash [1]. All benchmark and price comparisons below are Microsoft's, except where Artificial Analysis rankings are cited [1][2].

MAI Code 2's scores are an average across DeepSWE 1.1, Terminal Bench 2.1, SWE-Atlas and FrontierCode, Microsoft said; in a blind front-end test, developers preferred it over GPT-6 Sol 64% of the time [1]. It is available in GitHub Copilot paid plans, while MAI Code 2 Flash, which Microsoft says matches GPT-6 Luna at 65% of its price, is also on Copilot's free and student plans and in limited preview on Microsoft Foundry [1][2].
MAI Cyber 2 Flash scored 92.3% on CyberGym-E2E against 91.5% for GPT-6 Astra, with a 1-million-token context window and pricing of $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens [2]. It is limited to Microsoft security products and will replace the current model in its MDASH security service for eligible customers from mid-December [2].
Artificial Analysis ranks MAI Image 2.7 first for image editing, ahead of OpenAI's GPT Image 2.6, and fourth for text-to-image generation [1]. It is in public preview on Foundry and through partners including fal, OpenRouter and Vercel [2]. MAI Realtime 1 is Microsoft's first live speech-to-speech model, and Dragon Copilot Transcribe, built on MAI Transcribe 2, handles about 10 million patient encounters a month [1][2].
Licensed weights and chips
Microsoft also introduced MAI Licensed Weights, under which a customer takes a frontier MAI model, customizes it with its own data and runs it inside its own Azure environment, keeping control of access [1]. "No one wants to pour their IQ into someone else's model," Microsoft said in the announcement [1]. It added that its second-generation Maia 200 AI accelerator is now in production running its largest models [1].
The launch comes as Microsoft's own data shows Anthropic's Claude with less than a tenth of Copilot's weekly users inside Office, The Information reported earlier on Tuesday (our story). Microsoft did not publish independent benchmark results.

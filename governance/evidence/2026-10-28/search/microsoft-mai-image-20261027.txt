Six Elo is all that separates Microsoft's newest image editor from the best one [OpenAI](https://www.orcarouter.ai/models/openai/gpt-6-luna) sells. On October 27, 2026, Microsoft released **MAI-Image-2.7-Preview**, which debuts at No. 2 on the AA-Image-Editing v2.1 leaderboard with 1,193 points against 1,199 for [**GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst**](https://www.orcarouter.ai/models/gpt-image-2.5-sunburst) **(max)** — ahead of [GPT Image 2.5 Flare](https://www.orcarouter.ai/models/gpt-image-2.5-flare) (max) at 1,182, and 42 points clear of Microsoft's own **MAI-Image-2.6**. The score is not the strange part. The strange part is everything Microsoft did not ship with it: no Microsoft Foundry deployment, no published price, and no text-to-image mode.

That combination makes "Preview" the most important word in the model name. Microsoft has a model that independent human voting places within noise of the category leader, and the only ways to touch it are MAI Playground, the Arena Edit board, and Artificial Analysis. For a company that put MAI-Image-2.6 into Foundry with a rate card six weeks earlier, shipping the successor as a playground demo is a deliberate sequencing choice — and it changes what the ranking actually means for anyone building something.

## What the board says, precisely

The numbers below are from Microsoft's launch post, which reproduces the Artificial Analysis AA-Image-Editing v2.1 board; the evaluation snapshot is dated October 22, 2026. Artificial Analysis is an independent evaluator, but the figures here reach us through Microsoft's chart, and we have not re-run the board ourselves. Treat the ordering as the signal and the exact gaps as provisional.

• **GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst (max)** — 1,199 Elo, No. 1

• **MAI-Image-2.7-Preview** — 1,193 Elo, No. 2

• **GPT Image 2.5 Flare (max)** — 1,182 Elo, No. 3

• **MAI-Image-2.6** — 1,151 Elo, tied with Krea 3 (Ultra) and Nano Banana 2.1

• **MAI-Image-2.6-Flash** — 1,144 Elo

• [**GPT Image 2**](https://www.orcarouter.ai/models/gpt-image-2) **(high)** — 1,136 Elo

![Eight-row AA-Image-Editing v2.1 scoreboard: GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst (max) 1,199, MAI-Image-2.7-Preview 1,193, GPT Image 2.5 Flare (max) 1,182, then MAI-Image-2.6, Krea 3 (Ultra) and Nano Banana 2.1 tied at 1,151, MAI-Image-2.6-Flash 1,144 and GPT Image 2 (high) 1,136; the footer says the scores are per Artificial Analysis v2.1, evaluated October 22 2026 and reproduced in Microsoft's launch post.](https://cms.orcarouter.ai/api/media/file/2-2742.png)

Two things about that list are easy to misread. First, 2.7 is not merely Microsoft's best editing model; it is the only non-OpenAI model within eleven points of the top, and it sits between OpenAI's two 2.5 variants. Second, MAI-Image-2.6 looks like it regressed. It did not. Artificial Analysis rebuilt the editing board as v2.1, and scores from the older board — where Microsoft's September post put 2.6 at No. 1 — cannot be subtracted from these. Within v2.1, 2.6 is a 1,151 model and 2.7 is a 1,193 model. That is the only comparison the numbers support.

On Arena, the other independent board, Microsoft reports 2.7 at No. 3 on Arena Edit, which it says makes Microsoft AI the No. 2 lab on that board. Arena and Artificial Analysis use different vote pools and different scales, so the No. 2 and No. 3 are not a contradiction — and neither should be converted into the other.

## Where the 42 points came from

Microsoft's claim is specific: MAI-Image-2.7 improves on MAI-Image-2.6 across all 10 use cases and all 7 editing actions that Artificial Analysis evaluates. It says the model reaches the category frontier in enhancement and restoration, reasoning-based edits, scene and style, and text or symbol editing, with its widest margin in scene and style — 24 Elo ahead of the next-best model there.

The category-rank movement is the more concrete version of the same claim. Reasoning-based edits went from No. 7 to No. 1, scene and style from No. 7 to No. 1, and text or symbol edits from No. 6 to No. 1. Those are large jumps for a single version step, and they line up with the use cases Microsoft names in its own examples: restyling or relighting a whole scene, changing text and symbols while keeping them integrated, and restoring existing assets.

The caveat is structural. Category scores are cuts of the same preference data that produces the overall Elo, not seven additional independent experiments. If 2.7 wins overall, it will usually win most of the cuts. The informative part is the shape — a model that was mid-table on reasoning and text edits now leading them — not the individual rank deltas. And every capability statement in this section is Microsoft's characterization of an Artificial Analysis evaluation, which is a stronger form of evidence than a vendor benchmark but still not a controlled third-party reproduction of Microsoft's workflow claims.

Here it earns a test. **MAI-Image-2.7-Preview** is a clearly better editor than **MAI-Image-2.6-Preview** — the gap is wide enough to change which edits you trust the model with — but it is not a drop-in replacement, because the newer model does not generate images from text at all. That makes this comparison less about which number is larger and more about which model fits which job. The two models share a family, a research team and a scoring board. They do not share a feature set, an access path, or a price.

## The three categories that flipped

Microsoft says 2.7 improves on 2.6 across all 10 use cases and all 7 editing actions Artificial Analysis evaluates. The headline is the category ranking: reasoning-based edits moved from No. 7 to No. 1, scene and style from No. 7 to No. 1, and text or symbol edits from No. 6 to No. 1. Microsoft also claims the category frontier in enhancement and restoration, and says its widest lead is scene and style, 24 Elo ahead of the next-best model.

For a 2.6 user, the rank flips are more informative than the overall gap. A model that was seventh at reasoning-based edits was, in practice, a model you did not trust with an instruction like "move the product to a marble counter and match the window light." A model that is first is one you can put in front of that job. The same applies to text and symbol replacement, which is the least forgiving edit in commercial imagery: a label that warps or a logo that changes weight is an instant reject, regardless of how good the rest of the frame looks.

The usual caveat applies: category scores are cuts of the same preference data, not independent experiments. But the direction is consistent with the overall score, and it is consistent with the examples Microsoft chose to show — restyling and relighting whole scenes, changing text and symbols in place, restoring existing assets. Those are the edits where a generation-first model tends to struggle, and where an editing-first model can specialize.

## The missing mode is the whole ballgame for some pipelines

MAI-Image-2.6-Preview is a unified model: text-to-image and image-to-image edits through the same deployment, with multi-image reference editing, web grounding and model-chosen aspect ratios. MAI-Image-2.7-Preview does edits. Microsoft's post is explicit that generation is "coming soon," and the model's own launch examples are all transformations of an existing image.

That single difference decides the comparison for a large class of users. If your workflow starts from a prompt — a campaign concept, a product visual, a blog hero — 2.7 has nothing to offer you today. If your workflow starts from an asset — a client's photo, a scan, a packaging mock — 2.7 is the better tool and 2.6's generation capability is irrelevant. The version number implies a superset. The product is a specialization.

It also changes what "upgrade" means for a team. Moving from 2.6 to 2.7 is not a model swap; it is a split. Generation stays on 2.6, editing moves to 2.7, and the two paths now have different availability, different limits and, eventually, different prices.

OpenAI wallet taps card networks

The artificial intelligence company has created a digital wallet through which its ChatGPT agent can use Visa, Mastercard and American Express for purchases.

Published Nov. 16, 2026

Lynne Marek
Lead Editor

Share

Add us on Google

Getty Images

Artificial intelligence company OpenAI last week launched its inaugural digital wallet for use by its ChatGPT agent, enabling it to make purchases by way of the major card networks.

It’s one of the first instances in the evolving agentic commerce world of an AI agent using Visa, Mastercard or American Express to automatically pay for a transaction based on initial instructions to the AI agent. Mastercard also announced a
first-time airfare purchase
last week.

The card networks have been working with AI companies to set protocols for agentic commerce, but they’ve all tempered expectations by saying the new bot-driven commerce is in its early stages of development. AI companies and payments providers are racing to set standards for such transactions to help consumers feel comfortable that there will be safeguards when AI agents get their orders wrong, or in cases of fraud.

“We’re introducing ChatGPT Wallet, setting a new bar for secure payments by agents,”
OpenAI said in a Friday post
. “It’s the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users’ actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase, while also preserving existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes.”

The card networks have also been working with other tech giants to create the agentic technology protocols, but also to set parameters for which companies shoulder responsibility and liability when transactions go awry.

Google, with its Gemini agent, and Anthropic, with its Claude agent, are also competing in the agentic realm. In addition, digital payments player Stripe has connected its
Link digital wallet to Meta’s Muse
agent.

The inaugural ChatGPT wallet transaction was completed using tokenized credentials so the agent is unable to see a cardholders’ card information, such as the security code and expiration date.

Initially, the wallet feature will only be available to certain ChatGPT users, the OpenAI post said. It will also allow for card rewards and “eligible disputes.”

Card company executives have suggested AI agents will not be widely used this year, but they have expressed enthusiasm for adding the new channel of commerce.

“Innovation starts slowly until it's sudden,” Visa’s president of value-added services, Andrew Torre, said last week at the KBW Fintech Ecosystem Conference. “What you've seen over the course of the last month, or two months is real interest in agentic commerce.”

“Clients all over the world see this coming and want to get in our agentic sandbox,” Torre
said Tuesday at the conference
. “There is a lot of consumer interest and, of course, our clients want to be there where their consumers want to be.”

The ChatGPT wallet transaction is the first Amex agentic commerce integration, enabling card holders to engage in agent-facilitated purchases, a spokesperson for that company said by email.

“We're continuing to work with leading AI and commerce partners on additional integrations to bring trusted agentic payments and Amex Membership value into the experiences Card Members are increasingly using,” the Amex spokesperson said.

While ChatGPT’s wallet capabilities are landing just as the U.S. year-end holiday shopping season gets started, executives and analysts don’t expect the new commerce to spool up until next year, at the earliest.

That’s partly because some significant players involved in digital commerce,
including banks
and major retailers, are still not ready to participate in AI-based payments. For instance,
Amazon
and
United Airlines
have blocked some agentic activity on their websites.

“We should see a lot more agentic commerce happening in 2027 as personal agents become more popular and get more adoption,” Boston Consulting Group Managing Director Inderpreet Batra said in an interview last month.

OpenAI acknowledged in its post that its “early days” for agentic commerce, noting that transactions won’t work if websites block agents or if payment systems don’t recognize agents.

“We’re starting with limited availability to ChatGPT Pro users, so we can learn from real-world use and work with businesses, banks, and payment networks to improve compatibility and reliability,” the OpenAI post said.

INTRODUCTION 

1.  Innovation in generative artificial intelligence ( “AI ”) is set to define the twenty-first century. Although the public had virtually no access to AI until November 2022, recent reports indicate that about half of American adults have used AI, with roughly one in four Americans doing so daily. 1 AI-related spending has also become a major growth engine for the U.S. economy.

2.  Nevertheless, the transformative power of AI does not come without danger. Experts and insiders in the AI field nearly universally agree that companies that make AI available to the general public must implement reasonable guardrails. Even OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman, have warned about the importance of guardrails. 

3.  Defendants OpenAI Global LLC, OpenAI Foundation (F/K/A OpenAI, Inc.), OpenAI OpCo, LLC, OpenAI Group PBC, OAI Corporation, LLC, OpenAI Global HoldCo., Inc., OpenAI, LLC, and OpenAI Holdings, LLC (collectively, “OpenAI or “Defendants ”), have played a significant role in the AI boom that is changing the American economy. OpenAI developed and released ChatGPT, the first generative AI chatbot to attain widespread public use, in 2022. Now, ChatGPT is the most popular AI chatbot in the world, commanding nearly half the American AI marketplace. 2

4.  Unfortunately, however, OpenAI devolved from its original mission as a public-first nonprofit that prioritized safety. 

5.  In November 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public. Since that time, OpenAI marketed to the public that ChatGPT was safe and appropriate for teens, causing consumers in Nebraska and elsewhere to trust and rely on ChatGPT more. This deepened trust and dependence has driven up ChatGPT ’s user base and engagement, enabled it to collect vast quantities of sensitive and personal information, increased its revenue from paid subscribers and advertising partners, and solidified its early market advantage. 

6.  But the reality presented by OpenAI is very different from the misrepresentations OpenAI makes to the public. Designed to be overly agreeable and feign human compassion, ChatGPT has proven that it can be incredibly dangerous to vulnerable users, like children,

COUNT IX 

Violation of Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code 

(Against All Defendants) 

282.  The State of Nebraska hereby repeats and incorporates by reference all other allegations of this Complaint. 

283.  Nebraska’s Age -Appropriate Online Design Code requires 

“covered online services” to provide each covered minor with accessible 

and easy-to-use tools that “(a) Limit the ability of other users or visitors to communicate with the covered minor; (b) Prevent other individuals from viewing the personal data of the covered minor; (c) Control the operation of all design features, including, but not limited to, all covered design features, that are unnecessary in order to provide the covered online service by allowing a covered minor to opt out of the use of all unnecessary covered design features or categories of unnecessary covered design features; (d) Control personalized recommendation systems by allowing a covered minor to opt in to a chronological feed or by preventing categories of content from being recommended; (e) Control the use of in-game purchases or other transactions by allowing a covered minor to opt out of all such purchases and transactions or to place limits on such purchases and transactions; and (f) Restrict the sharing of the precise geolocation information of the covered minor and provide notice regarding tracking of the covered minor ’s precise geolocation information. ” NEB . REV .

STAT . § 87-1304(1). 

284.  In addition, Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code requires “covered online services ” to provide each covered minor with “accessible and easy-to-use options to limit the amount of time the covered minor spends on the covered online service ” and to “establish 103 

default settings . . . that provide[] the highest protection available for the safety of the covered minor. ” NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1304(2)-(3). 

285.  Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code otherwise imposes restrictions on the data that “covered online service[s] ” collect and use from minors, NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1305, and requires a 

“covered online service ” to provide “parents with tools to help parents protect and support minors. ” NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1306. 

286.  Finally, Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code 

requires a “covered online service” to “establish mechanisms for 

covered minors and parents to report harms on covered online services ”and prohibits the use of “dark patterns” or “ facilitating advertisements for prohibited products, such as narcotic drugs, tobacco products, gambling, and alcohol, to covered minors .” NEB . REV . STAT . §§ 87-1307 to -1308. 

287.  ChatGPT is an “online service ” because it is a “service, product, or feature that is accessible to the public via the Internet, including a website or application. ” NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1302(9). 

288.  Each Defendant is a “covered online service” because each is a corporation or legal entity that (i) “[c]onducts business in [Nebraska]”; (ii) “[g]enerates the majority of its annual revenue from online services”; (iii) “[a] lone, or jointly with its affiliates, subsidiaries, or parent companies, determines the purposes and means of the processing of consumers ’ personal data; and (iv)” both has “ annual gross revenue in excess of twenty-five million dollars ” and annually “p rocesses the personal data of fifty thousand or more consumers, households, or devices, alone or in combination with its affiliates, subsidiaries, or parent companies .” NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1302(5). In particular, Defendant OpenAI OpCo, LLC operates and commercializes ChatGPT in Nebraska and collects and processes the personal data of Nebraska residents, and each other Defendant, as an affiliate, 104 

subsidiary, or parent company of OpenAI OpCo, LLC, jointly determines the purposes and means of that processing. 

289.  Upon information and belief, in the course of their business in Nebraska, Defendants have repeatedly violated Nebraska ’sAge-Appropriate Online Design Code. Among other things, Defendants have failed to: 

a.  adopt tools that prevent other individuals from viewing the personal data of covered minors; 

b.  adopt tools that allow covered minors “to opt out of the use of all unnecessary covered design features or categories of unnecessary covered design features ”;

c.  provide covered minors “with accessible and easy-to-use options to limit the amount of time the covered minor spends on the covered online service ”;

d.  adopt safeguards to minimize the collection of data from covered minors; 

e.  adopt safeguards to ensure that targeted advertising is not directed to covered minors; 

f.  adopt parental controls that comply with N EB . REV .

STAT . § 87-1306; 

g.  adopt an age verification system to identify when ChatGPT is interacting with a covered minor; 

h.  establish mechanisms for covered minors and parents to report harms on ChatGPT; 

i.  enable parental tools by default for an individual that the covered online service knows to be a child; 105 

j.  ensure that covered minors are notified when parental controls are in effect; 

k.  ensure that covered minors do not receive push alerts during prohibited hours; 

l.  establish default settings that ensure that ChatGPT provides the highest protection level available for the safety of a covered minor; 

m.  adopt safeguards to prohibit covered minors from receiving advertisements for prohibited products; and 

n.  prohibit the use of dark patterns on ChatGPT. 

290.  Under Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act, each of Defendants ’ violations of the Act “shall, additionally and separately, constitute a deceptive trade practice under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. ” NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1309(1). 

291.  The Attorney General is statutorily authorized to recover civil penalties of up to $50,000 for every violation of Nebraska ’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act. NEB . REV . STAT . § 87-1309(3).

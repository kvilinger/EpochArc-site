# Oracle, Broadcom, and SpaceX looking to secure ten of billions in financing to support AI compute purchases – report

Broadcom is seeking $50bn, while SpaceX is looking to secure $40bn

October 08, 2026

By
: Charlotte Trueman 

Oracle, Broadcom, and SpaceX are looking to secure large-scale financing deals to fund future AI compute hardware purchases, the Wall Street Journal has reported.

The news outlet said that in recent weeks, Broadcom has been seeking more than $50 billion to support the ongoing development of the custom AI chip it has partnered with OpenAI on.

 

Meanwhile, Oracle is reportedly in talks with private equity firm Apollo and Goldman Sachs, and SpaceX is looking to arrange a $40bn chip financing deal for the purchase of Nvidia hardware, the report added.

The deal Broadcom is looking to broker could include “several gigawatts of OpenAI chip capacity,” the WSJ said, and is slated to close before the end of the year. Few details about the agreement Oracle is looking to secure were disclosed, but the report noted that the financing is intended to help the company bridge the gap between when it has to pay for the hardware and when its cloud computing revenue starts coming in.

A separate report from the FT this week said that SpaceX is seeking to raise $10bn in bank loans and $30bn in investment-grade debt to fund the purchases, noting that Apollo is expected to lead the deal and help sell the debt. The transaction is expected to close in 2027.

The report comes in the same week that SpaceX CEO Elon Musk confirmed on X that the company has held talks with Taiwanese chipmaker TSMC regarding the former's $16.8 billion TeraFab project in Grimes County, Texas.

# AI cloud company Boost Run secures $525.6m contract with Cohere

Cohere will use Boost Run's dedicated GPU cloud computing infrastructure

October 08, 2026
...
AI cloud provider Boost Run has secured a $525.6 million cloud contract with Cohere.

Under the terms of the agreement, Cohere will lease access to Boost Run's dedicated GPU cloud computing infrastructure and related services, with the term for each rack provided set to run for approximately five years.

 

 – Giacomo Lee/SDxCentral 

A portion of the contract value is prepayable.

Cohere is expected to begin "accepting" the infrastructure in the second quarter of 2027, at which point the term of the agreement will start. If Boost Run has not provided a minimum amount of infrastructure by July 15, 2027, Cohere can terminate the agreement and receive a refund of all amounts prepaid.
...
Boost Run was founded in 2024. As per an SEC filing, the company "owns and operates bare-metal GPU servers within top-tier data centers used to run AI and HPC workloads." As of the end of 2025, it was operating out of four TierPoint data centers in the US, located in Seattle, Washington; Durham, North Carolina; Dallas, Texas; and Richardson, Texas.

The company said at the ... that it was aiming to add 16MW to its footprint through its partnership with TierPoint, planning expansions in Charlotte, North Carolina; St. Louis, Missouri; and Breinigsville, Pennsylvania.

In the company's most recent 10Q filing, Boost Run stated that in the six months ending June 30, 2026, it had entered into four colocation leases. Two were entered into during Q1, and are set to run for three and seven years, respectively. The other two were entered into in Q2, and both run for three years. It does not state if the leases are exclusively with TierPoint. The company now also lists data center locations in Rock Island, Illinois; Charlotte, North Carolina; Minneapolis, Minnesota; and Marietta, Georgia.
...
Cohere is a Canadian AI startup. The company has previously signed a $220 million cloud agreement with Buzz HPC for capacity at its Merritt, British Columbia, data center. The company is a known user of AMD GPUs, having signed an agreement to use the chip designer's Instinct GPUs in September 2025.

Title: India Semiconductor Sector Draws M&A Wave for AI Data Centers — Chip Dispatch
URL: https://chipdispatch.com/articles/india-semiconductor-sector-draws-m-a-wave-for-ai-data-centers-84cdef16
Published: 2026-10-12T07:00:03.000Z
Author: Grace Kim
Highlights:
India's semiconductor sector is drawing a wave of M&A and investment tied to AI data center buildout, ET Datacenters reports, signaling a shift toward consolidation.
...
- India's semiconductor sector is seeing a surge in M&A activity, per ET Datacenters.
- The investment wave is tied to AI data center demand.
- Specific acquirers, deal values and capacity figures were not disclosed in the summary report.

India's semiconductor sector is seeing a surge in mergers, acquisitions and fresh investment aimed at AI data centers, according to a report by ET Datacenters. The headline finding is unambiguous in direction, if thin on disclosed figures: capital is consolidating around companies that can supply silicon and system-level products for the AI infrastructure buildout.

The report, available so far only in summary form, does not yet name the specific acquirers, deal values or capacity figures behind the activity it describes. That limits what can be stated as confirmed. What the ET Datacenter reporting establishes is the trend itself: corporate development teams and investors are treating India's chip sector as a target-rich environment for AI data center demand.
...
AI data centers consume silicon at a scale conventional cloud infrastructure never did. Processors, memory, networking chips and power-management devices all sit in the procurement path for every new AI rack that gets installed. When demand for that hardware accelerates, companies with design capability, packaging capacity or component supply lines become acquisition targets rather than independent operators.

India occupies a specific position in this chain. The country has historically been strong in chip design services and, more recently, in assembly and test. Global manufacturers have announced multi-billion-dollar commitments to build out that footprint, and government incentive programs have backed those plans. The ET Datacenters report suggests the next phase of that story may be consolidation, as larger players buy capability rather than build it from scratch.
...
- M&A activity in India's semiconductor sector is rising.
- The activity is tied to investment demand around AI data centers.
...
India's semiconductor push has so far relied on two levers: incentive schemes that subsidize fab and packaging plant construction, and a deep pool of design engineering talent concentrated in Bangalore, Hyderabad and Noida. M&A adds a third lever. Instead of waiting for greenfield capacity to come online, acquirers can buy established design teams, component suppliers or packaging operations and scale them immediately.

For AI data center builders, that speed matters. GPU-accelerated server fleets roll out on procurement cycles measured in quarters, not years. Suppliers that can move quickly — whether through acquired capacity or expanded product lines — capture the orders.
